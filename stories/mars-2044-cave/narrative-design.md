# Narrative Design

## Narrative anchor

On Sol 11 of the first substantial human occupation of Ares Colony, Mei Zhang
leads a survey into terrain prepared by autonomous construction machines and
finds evidence that both the machines and the humans are late arrivals to a
site of unknown intelligence.

The story should feel like hard-SF field procedure tightening into a first
ethical encounter with the unknown.

## POV and lens

- Primary viewpoint: close third on Mei Zhang.
- Narrative distance: tight enough to keep the geology, EVA procedure, and
  shifting inference tied to Mei's judgement.
- What this lens lets the story notice: material evidence, operational risk,
  and the moral difference between explanation and possession.

## Dramatic question

Can Mei determine what kind of mistake or discovery lies inside the cave before
the colony's need for expansion turns uncertainty into damage?

## Thematic question

What kind of colony proves itself worthy of a new world: one that exploits an
unknown quickly, or one that learns how to stop and witness?

## Ending logic

- What must be resolved: the reason the construction fleet omitted the site
  from the human maps.
- What should remain open: the origin of the basalt object and the meaning of
  the fresh score beside it.
- What final note or image should the story land on: Mei choosing protection
  over expansion, followed by a last physical sign that keeps the site alive in
  the reader's mind.

## Reveal structure and clue ladder

1. First signal: the ridge corridor is physically present but absent from the
   inherited expansion maps.
2. Escalation: scored basalt and a hidden witness marker prove the omission was
   deliberate rather than clerical.
3. Reframe or reversal: recovered machine memory shows the fleet quarantined
   the site autonomously and encountered a manufactured basalt object it did
   not create.
4. Final reveal boundary: a fresh narrow score beside the object prevents the
   story from closing the question of agency.

## Drift risks and handoff notes

- Preserve the colony's material pressure for eastward ground so caution always
  has a cost.
- Keep Idris as a sincere defender of machine rationality rather than a foil to
  be embarrassed.
- Avoid mystical rhetoric; every major shift should come from observed physical
  evidence, recovered machine behaviour, or a concrete operational decision.
- Revision passes should re-check that the closing image remains physical and
  restrained rather than essayistic.
