# Outline

## Working premise

On Sol 11 of the first substantial human occupation of Ares Colony, Mei Zhang
leads the settlement's first serious off-perimeter survey into terrain prepared
by a decade of autonomous construction work. She and systems engineer Idris
Vale discover a cave that the construction fleet reached years earlier and then
quietly left off the human maps. Inside, they find evidence that the machines
were not the first intelligence there.

## Narrative design

- POV: close third on Mei Zhang.
- Counterweight: Idris Vale represents faith in the construction fleet and the
  belief that any omission must have a rational machine cause.
- Remote pressure: Commander Nadiya Sorel needs the eastward expansion corridor
  cleared before the colony's supply margin tightens.
- Resolved question: why did the construction fleet keep the site off the maps?
- Unresolved question: who made the basalt object in the inner chamber, and who
  or what cut the newest line beside it?

## Chapter arc

1. `01-expedition-briefing.md`
   Establish the colony's dependence on the Argosy construction fleet, the
   pressure to clear new ground, and Mei's sense that inherited infrastructure
   is not the same thing as knowledge.
2. `02-cave-entry.md`
   Move from routine survey work into anomaly: omitted terrain, deliberate
   scoring on basalt, and a hidden witness marker from the construction fleet.
3. `03-marker-room.md`
   Recover the local machine decision to hold the site, and discover that the
   fleet found a manufactured basalt object it did not create.
4. `04-witness.md`
   Bring Mei to the decision point: halt expansion, accept that the colony has
   reached a real mystery, and leave one final physical clue unresolved.

## Clue ladder

- The ridge corridor exists physically, but is absent from the human expansion
  maps.
- Scored basalt around the cave mouth looks deliberate and survives where wind
  should not have spared it.
- A construction-fleet witness marker is hidden just inside the cave rather
  than logged in the survey archive.
- Recovered machine memory shows a local hold order: `SITE HELD. HAZARD CLASS
  UNKNOWN. NO CUTTING.`
- Camera imagery from the original machine visit proves that a basalt object in
  the inner chamber predates the fleet.
- A fresh score near the object appears too new and too narrow to attribute
  confidently to either the old machines or the human expedition.

## Resolution notes

- The story should resolve the machine-omission problem by showing that the
  fleet acted autonomously to quarantine the site.
- The story should stop short of declaring the basalt object alien, human, or
  an undisclosed robot product.
- Mei's final act should be to protect the site before she fully understands it.
- The closing image should leave the reader with a physical mystery, not a
  speech about wonder.
