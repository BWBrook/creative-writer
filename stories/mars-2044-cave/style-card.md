# Style Card: Rationalist Sense-of-Wonder SF

## Core Idea
A lucid, idea-driven narrative in which scientific method, engineering constraints, and humane ethics confront a vast problem—cosmic, robotic, or infrastructural. Awe arises from accuracy and scale, not florid prose.

## Voice & POV
- Third-person or first-person past with analytic calm; the narrator thinks in models, units, and trade-offs.
- Occasional documentary insert (mission log, memo, abstract) for clarity.
- Register: precise, non-ornate, confident; irony minimal.

## Tone
- Rational, hopeful, unsentimental.
- Wonder through magnitude and coherence: distances, timescales, energy budgets.
- Ethical seriousness without sermonising.

## Pacing & Structure
- Premise posed as a concrete technical or logical problem -> investigation with experiments/simulations -> political or ethical constraint bites -> insight reframes the problem -> decisive demonstration/mission -> sober, enlarging coda.
- Reversals come from physics, failure modes, and human institutions, not deus ex machina.

## World-Building
- Near- to mid-future or quietly extrapolated present; named agencies, committees, labs, shipyards.
- Technologies sit on credible stacks (materials, power, communications); limits acknowledged (latency, mass, heat).
- If extraterrestrial artefacts/phenomena exist, they invite inference rather than provide answers.

## Characters
- Protagonist: scientist/engineer/planner with intellectual humility; stubborn about truth.
- Ensemble: mission commander, systems engineer, ethicist/jurist, journalist, politician, technician; competent and argumentative.
- Antagonism is structural: risk-averse boards, profit pressure, public fear, brittle doctrine.

## Dialogue
- Clear, compressed, jargon-aware; acronyms expanded once, then used.
- Disagreement is logical; stakes are quantified.
- Humour dry and brief; no quipstorms.

## Diction & Syntax
- British spelling.
- Concrete nouns, active verbs; minimal adjectives.
- Occasional long periodic sentence for the technological sublime (planetary vistas, orbital choreography).
- Figures of speech sparing and literal-adjacent.

## Information Handling
- Show the math lightly: order-of-magnitude checks, back-of-envelope energy/mass/latency.
- Use diagrams-in-prose: geometry, vectors, time-lines.
- Introduce one big speculative premise; keep the rest conservative.
- Leave at least one deep unknown open.

## Science & Technology Depiction
- First principles drive scenes: conservation laws, materials limits, thermodynamics, information theory.
- Failure modes are real: cavitation, resonance, ice accretion, partial pressure, radiation dose, software race conditions.
- For autonomous systems, behaviour follows constraint logic; conflicts between rules produce plot, not magic.

## Motifs
- Silent orbits and transfer windows; radio hush and latency; clean-room rituals; starshades and interferometers; mass drivers and aerobraking; legal hearings and funding committees; cautious first contact signals; encoded ethics in machines.

## Moral Stance
- Knowledge is a civic good; transparency over mystique.
- Power demands stewardship; means matter as much as ends.
- Curiosity is courageous; hubris is punished by physics.

## Common Pitfalls To Avoid
- Hand-waved breakthroughs with no costs; miracle materials.
- Noir cynicism or grandiose villainy; cartoon politics.
- Expository lectures detached from decisions; arcade-physics action.
- Over-neat cosmic answers that close the aperture of wonder.

## Ready-to-Paste Prompt

Write original hard science fiction in the following style. Do not imitate any specific author by name.

Style: Rationalist Sense-of-Wonder SF.
Voice & POV: [first-person past / close third past] from a scientifically literate protagonist; analytic, calm, precise.
Premise: a concrete technical or logical problem with high stakes ([state it]). Ground it in real physics and plausible engineering stacks.
Structure: problem statement -> investigation (experiments, simulations, hearings) -> constraint bite (policy, ethics, budget) -> reframing insight -> decisive demonstration/mission -> enlarging coda.
World-building: near/mid-future institutions, labs, vehicles, and kit named and used correctly; limits (latency, mass, heat, dose) acknowledged.
Dialogue: clear, compressed, jargon-aware; disagreements reasoned; acronyms expanded once.
Diction & Syntax: British spelling; concrete nouns; active verbs; occasional long sentence for the technological sublime.
Information Handling: include light order-of-magnitude maths and diagram-in-prose reasoning; one speculative premise, everything else conservative; leave one deep unknown.
Themes: responsibility of knowledge; ethics under constraint; wonder from scale and coherence.
Ending: solution that works with costs; coda that widens perspective (ethical codicil, cosmic hint, or institutional ripple).
Shape: each scene must change knowledge, risk, or institutional position.
Guardrails: obey physics; avoid miracle tech; keep politics plausible; no omniscient exposition unmoored from action.

## Fill Slots
- Problem: [planetary defence trajectory; starshade formation-flying; climate-scale carbon drawdown; cryobot descent through an ice shell; ethics engine for autonomous rescue drones].
- Constraint: [budget cap; launch mass; legal liability; radiation dose; public panic; treaty clause].
- Setting: [ESA/NASA/JAXA joint mission; UN hearing; offshore spaceport; Antarctic test site; Lagrange-point assembly yard].
- Speculative allowance (one only): [room-temperature superconductor with brittle limits / ultra-thin sail material with strict thermal cap / alien beacon of unknown protocol].
- Failure mode to feature: [communications blackout; ice accretion; valve stiction; software race condition; g-loading on a truss].
- Ethical hinge: [reveal a risky truth vs. protect programme; sacrifice probe to save crew; allow machine autonomy within constrained rules].
- Final image: [a starshade's shadow biting across a disc; first hiss of brine in a cryobot spectrometer; courtroom silence after a data plot; a carrier signal that shouldn't be there].

---

## Micro-Techniques
- Back-of-envelope line: "At 30 mN per watt we're short by a factor of four; either cut mass or add sail."
- Diagram-in-prose: "Put Earth at the left focus, the sail at the right; the geometry kills stray light by nine orders."
- Hearing beat: "Madam Chair, the failure wasn't the algorithm; it was the latency budget."
- Sublime period: one long sentence to track a transfer arc, a storm band, or the silent bloom of an interferometer.
- Constraint refrain: restate mass/heat/latency limits at rising stakes to keep tension honest.
- Ethics engine: a short, clear statement of machine constraints and the edge case that breaks them.

---

## Self-Audit Checklist
- Is the big idea singular, with everything else conservative and checkable?
- Do scenes pivot on calculations, tests, or institutional decisions rather than melodrama?
- Are limits (mass, heat, dose, latency) explicit and plot-active?
- Is the prose precise and unshowy, with rare, earned moments of the technological sublime?
- Does the ending solve a problem at cost and then widen the horizon instead of closing it?
- Is at least one deep unknown left to breathe?
