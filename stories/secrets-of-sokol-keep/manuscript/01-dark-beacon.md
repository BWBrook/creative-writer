The beacon had been dark for two nights, and by the third evening the docks had
begun to look for a face to put on the trouble.

Keria watched that happen from a booth at the Laughing Goblin, with her back to
the wall and her cup untouched. The tavern was old timber, yellow plaster,
rusted iron, cabbage steam, wet wool, and smoke. Above the common room a second
gallery leaned over the noise. Sailors spoke too loudly under it. Dockworkers
kept turning their heads toward the door. Every man who came in looked first at
the two mercenaries in the corner.

Aravele noticed it too. She had a way of resting one hand near her belt without
seeming to reach for anything. "They've picked our corner."

"Grim wore the same colour," Keria said.

"Grim owes half this room money."

"That too."

Across the room, Imizael moved behind the bar with a rag in one hand and a
look that counted cups, knives, tempers, and exits. She had already told three
men to sit down and one to pay before speaking. The old carved sign above the
bar showed a laughing green face worn nearly smooth by smoke and hands.

Markoth Hasselpond came with two bowls of soup and a third bowl balanced on his
forearm for himself. His shirt was damp at the collar. His cheeks shone. He
set Keria's bowl down first because she had called him Markoth and not Fat Mar.

"There," he said. "Hot enough to kill what ails you. Unless what ails you is a
dark lighthouse, in which case I advise prayer, bribery, or leaving town."

Aravele took the spoon. "You have heard something."

Markoth sat without invitation. "I hear everything. It is why my feet hurt."

"Your profession is carrying soup."

"And witnesses." He leaned in. "Sokol Keep went dark two days ago. No signal,
no guard boat, no message, no apology. House Sokol says the Black Fist are
there to keep order. The Black Fist says House Sokol owns the beacon. The
ships say no light means no departure. The dockers say no departure means no
pay."

Keria drank. The ale was sour and thin. "What about Grim?"

Markoth's face changed by a small amount. Gossip was one thing. A named guard
was another. "Sergeant Thornbuck? He had the island detail."

"I know."

"Then you know he liked money before work and praise before either. He was
friendly with young Igan Sokol. More friendly than a guard sergeant needs to be
with a house quartermaster."

Aravele looked up. "Why?"

"Treasure." Markoth lowered his voice. "Old temple under the keep. Dead
priests. Buried gold. Same tales men tell when they want to dig on land they do
not own."

Keria saw Grim as he had been six weeks earlier, laughing with a cracked tooth
and promising to buy the table a round once his luck turned. He had owed for the
last round too. "Did he say he found it?"

"He said he was close."

The door opened hard enough to strike the wall.

Six dockworkers came in behind Thirus, a broad man with salt dried white in his
beard and a red mark across one cheek. They brought cold air and harbour anger.
The room quieted in pieces. Imizael stopped wiping the bar. Markoth stood with
his bowl still in his hand.

Thirus crossed straight toward Keria's booth. "There they are."

Aravele set her spoon down.

"Sit," Keria said.

"Make me."

Keria rose. She did it slowly, keeping her hands visible. Chairs scraped back.
Men leaned away from the tables to see.

"If you came for Grim, he is not here," she said.

"His lot are at the keep."

"Then go to the keep."

One of the younger men behind Thirus laughed. It came out badly. "No ferry
runs in the dark."

"Then wait for morning."

Thirus stepped closer. He smelled of beer and wet hemp. "My brother's boat sits
loaded because there is no light on Thorn Island. Two days of wages gone. If
your friend and his soldiers are drunk up there, I will have the cost from
someone."

Keria let the word friend pass. "You will get answers faster with your teeth
in your head."

His hand twitched. Aravele rose then, quiet as a hinge. Across the room,
Imizael said, "No blades."

Men heard her. Blades meant Black Fist questions and a body on the floor. Fists
were still tavern business.

Keria took her purse from her belt and put three coins on the table. "Your
first round is paid. Your second is paid if you tell me who last came from the
island."

Thirus stared at the coins. He had come in to hit someone. Three coins slowed
him down.

"Karst," he said. "Old ferryman. Brought lumber back empty yesterday. Said the
servants were alive. Said the guards were nowhere in sight."

"And Igan Sokol?"

"No one saw him."

No one spoke for a beat. A missing Sokol made the matter cleaner and worse.

Markoth put his bowl down. "House office opens at first bell. Liela keeps it
tidier than a priest's conscience. Hurn at the Black Fist post will give
permission to anyone who saves him walking."

Keria picked up her cup at last and drank the rest. Outside, beyond roof and
smoke and human anger, the harbour lay under a darkness that should have had a
light in it.

"Morning," Aravele said.

Keria nodded. "Morning."
