The keep was not empty. That made its quiet worse.

Keria had known empty buildings. This one was maintained. The yard had swept
stone paths, stacked timber, repaired hinges, and practice dummies scarred by
soldier work. The mansion wing smelled faintly of bread and lye. Lamps had oil
in them. A broom leaned against a wall where someone meant to return for it.

Shandra met them inside with Rorin behind her. She had keys on a cord and fear
held down by habit. "Sergeant Grim and his guards used the lighthouse floors.
Master Igan kept rooms above the courtyard. Darvag told you that much?"

"He did," Keria said.

"Then I will show you where he read."

Igan's quarters had a made bed, clean linen, and books on every flat surface.
Papers lay on the desk, bed, chair, and floor. There were sketches of tower
walls, old joints in masonry, shrine fronts, hands, scales, and one careful
drawing of a blind god with one hand missing. Poor verses had been crossed
through so hard the paper had torn.

Aravele lifted a page by one corner. "He was looking for something."

"He was always looking," Shandra said. "Then Sergeant Grim started looking with
him."

Keria found the hollow book by weight rather than cunning. It held a silver
holy symbol and a small pouch of platinum. She closed it again and set it back.
The room grew colder by a degree.

Aravele noticed. "Leave it?"

"Leave it."

On the bookcase, one volume sat forward from the others. It fell before Keria
touched it. The cover struck the floor and opened to a page marked by a dry
thread. The title was written in old Thorass and translated beneath by another
hand: Moonsea Cults.

Rorin crossed himself with a speed that had more habit than doctrine in it.

"Who moved that?" Keria asked.

Shandra did not answer.

The East Tower stood behind the mansion under dust and old cobweb. Furniture
had been dragged against one wall, then moved aside many times. The boards
behind it were loose at the bottom. Keria pulled one free, then another. Cold
air came through the gap.

Inside, the hidden shrine was cleaner than any unused room had a right to be.
A grey statue stood at the far end, blind and one-handed. Brass scales rested
on the altar. A purple-wood desk held a journal bound in worn leather.

Keria removed her glove before touching it.

The journal was not orderly. Igan had written as if each discovery made the
previous one urgent. Childhood memory. Harae. Books falling open. Grim's
confidence. West Tower. The phrase repeated in several hands, first uncertain,
then underlined until the nib had broken: Toe Mono, Toe Megalo.

"The One," Aravele said, reading over her shoulder. "The Great."

"He thought it opened something."

From the altar came one clear knock.

Nobody moved.

Keria looked at the statue. "Did Grim go below with him?"

Two knocks.

Aravele let out the breath through her nose. "Yes."

"Is Igan alive?"

The shrine stayed silent. Shandra lowered her eyes. Then the brass scales
trembled, tilted once, and stilled.

They found the West Tower by smell. Wet earth sat under old dust. In the
basement, someone had broken the floor and dug down to ancient stone. Tools lay
in order along the wall. Three coils of rope were stacked beside a ring fixed
into the masonry. One severed line led to a round slab ten feet across, bordered
with worn glyphs and a handprint hollow near the rim.

Aravele crouched by the cut rope. "Clean blade."

"From below or above?"

"Hard to tell."

Keria put Igan's journal beside the slab and read the phrase again. The glyphs
flickered without light. The air lifted the hair at her wrist.

"If this kills me," she said, "tell Hurn I objected."

"In writing," Aravele said.

Keria placed her hand in the hollow and spoke the words.

The slab vanished. No grinding, no dust, no hinge. One moment it was stone. The
next, a black shaft opened under her palm, breathing wet air from below.

Far beneath them, water dripped in the dark.
