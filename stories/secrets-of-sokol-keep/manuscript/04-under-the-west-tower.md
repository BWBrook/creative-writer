They tied two ropes before descending. Aravele made Keria test both knots.
Keria made Aravele test the ladder. Old magic could keep its own habits. Rope
and wood still had theirs.

The shaft dropped through five feet of stone into a low chamber. Mud covered
the tile floor in a thin skin. Boot prints crossed it toward an open brass
door. Above them, the underside of the vanished slab showed the same hand mark,
pale in lantern light.

The first thing Keria saw was the rope.

It lay coiled at the foot of the ladder, sliced clean through.

Aravele knelt and touched the end. "Sharp knife. No fray."

"Grim cut his own way out?"

"Or someone cut it for him."

Keria studied the boot tracks. Several men had entered. None had returned by
the mud she could read. The chamber smelled of wet stone, mildew, and brine
that had no business so far under the keep.

They passed through the brass door.

The temple narrowed into a corridor and turned sharply by a high alcove full of
calcified bones. Keria lifted the lantern. The bones had fused together under
mineral skin. No gold showed among them. No weapon. No answer.

"Leave them," she said.

"I was planning to."

The next room sloped while the ceiling held level. Old barnacles clung to wall,
floor, and carved pillars, white and grey under mud. Two suits of armour stood
in niches, crusted in coral and shell until the iron beneath showed only at the
joints. Nautilus helms bowed over empty gorgets.

Keria stopped before the middle of the room.

There was a fresh scrape in the mud between the niches.

"Back," she said.

The armour moved.

It did not leap. It tore itself free by inches, barnacles cracking, old shell
falling in dry flakes. One helm lifted. Empty darkness looked out.

Aravele threw the coil of spare rope low. Keria kicked the lantern stand toward
the far wall, giving them side light and shadow enough to see legs. The first
armour stepped into the rope and dragged it. Aravele pulled. Keria drove a
hammer spike between shell knee and stone joint.

The thing struck her shoulder. Pain ran down her arm into her hand. She kept
the spike in place and felt Aravele haul. The armour fell slowly, heavily, and
hit the floor with a sound like crockery dropped into a grave.

The second one came on through the noise.

They did not try to kill it. It had no life to offer them. They wedged the
fallen body across the narrowest passage, backed through the door beyond, and
shoved until old hinges screamed. The shell thing beat against brass three
times. After that it was only a dull knocking behind them.

Keria flexed her right hand. It obeyed, badly.

"Can you climb?" Aravele asked.

"Ask me when we reach climbing."

The next chamber had water to the shin. In the centre, a dark round hole
swallowed lantern light. A smear of dried blood marked the far door handle.
High in the eastern wall, a narrow opening showed black.

The tracks crossed the water, muddied and confused. One print had slid toward
the hole. A hand had clawed algae from the stone beside it.

Keria tied rope around her waist. Aravele took the other end and braced against
the door frame.

"Slow," Aravele said.

Keria went slow. The water numbed her boots and climbed through the seams. The
floor tilted without warning. Twice she stopped and let the silt settle. At the
far side she found the blood mark, the stuck door, and a strip of black cloth
caught in the hinge.

Black Fist cloth.

From somewhere ahead came a sound that might have been water, breath, or a man
trying not to speak.

Keria set her wet shoulder against the door. "They came this way."

"Still want Grim alive?"

Keria pushed until the old brass shifted.

"I want him found."
