The idol chamber was round, vaulted, and sick with green light.

The light came from the far side, where a figure of pale jade stood in a pool
of black water. It had the body of a man twisted toward the sea and the head of
a fish with too many teeth. Tentacles ran from its face into the carved base.
Old coins lay in the water around it. Bones made low white banks by the pool.

On the altar before it, a body lay folded over stone.

Keria knew Igan Sokol only from his rooms: ink, paper, earnest sketches, a
hidden shrine kept clean. On the altar he was no longer rumour or family name.
He had been young. The back of his coat was stiff with blood. The wound sat
under the left shoulder, narrow and deep.

Aravele looked once and then watched the room. "From behind."

"Yes."

The bones began to move.

They rose first at the pool edge, hands under water, ribs clicking against
stone. A corpse in Sokol clothing lifted its head from the altar. Igan's face
had gone waxen and slack. His eyes opened with no one behind them.

Keria swore once, quietly.

They gave ground by inches and took it back by inches. Aravele held the steps
with spear and shield while Keria dragged Igan's body down from the altar. The
dead pressed toward the green light. The air thickened around the idol. Each
breath took more work than it should. The cut in Keria's shoulder burned with
cold.

Then the lantern flame bent sideways though there was no wind. Keria remembered
the brass scales in the shrine.

"Away from the altar," she said.

Aravele understood. They pulled Igan's body from the raised stones. The dead
stumbled as if a cord had slackened. Keria struck one skeleton hard enough to
break the wrist. Aravele drove the butt of her spear into another and kicked it
down the steps. Bone scattered into the black pool.

The chamber still wanted them dead. It gave them room enough to move.

The reliquary door beyond the idol chamber had partly collapsed. Lamplight
showed through a gap at the base. Human lamplight, low and yellow, nearly gone.

"Grim," Keria called.

Something moved inside. A man's voice answered after too long. "Keria?"

The name hurt more than it should have.

"Come out."

"There are dead things out there."

"Fewer now."

"Igan woke them. He touched the idol. We tried to stop him."

Aravele looked down at Igan's body. Keria kept her eyes on the gap. "Come out
with your hands empty."

Grim crawled through first. He had lost weight in two days. His beard was
filthy, his lips cracked, and his eyes went everywhere except the body. Three
guards followed him, hollow-faced and shaking. One carried a sack until
Aravele's spear lowered toward it. He dropped it at once.

Coins spilled. Coloured glass. Small gold things. Beaten brass plates marked
with old glyphs.

Grim saw Keria see them.

"We took them after," he said. "Payment for surviving."

"After Igan was stabbed?"

"The dead killed him."

"The wound is in his back."

"The room was chaos."

Keria stepped closer. "He wanted the idol destroyed."

One of the guards made a sound. Grim turned on him.

Aravele moved to the guard before Grim could speak. "Say it once and be done."

The man looked at the floor. "Igan said it was evil. Said House Sokol had no
right. Grim called him a fool."

"And then?"

The man swallowed. "Then the sergeant used his knife."

Grim lunged for him. Keria caught him across the mouth with the iron pommel of
her knife. He went down hard in the mud and stayed on his hands, breathing
through blood.

For a moment she wanted to finish it there. The idol watched with its green
stone face. Frost silvered the buckle at Grim's throat.

Keria took Grim's belt knife, then his sword. She bound his hands with rope
taken from his own pack.

"You will walk," she said.

He spat red into the mud. "You think Hurn will care?"

"No."

She looked at Igan's body, the brass plates, the sacks, the guards, the wound
under the shoulder.

"That is why we are taking everything."
