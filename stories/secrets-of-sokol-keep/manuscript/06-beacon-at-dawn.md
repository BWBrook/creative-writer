They brought Igan up first.

It took time. Nothing in the old temple came up easily. Rope had to be set and
reset. The sinkhole took one of Grim's boots and nearly took a guard with it.
The shell armour beat once against the brass door as they passed, then fell
silent. In the entry chamber, Keria looked again at the cut rope and made Grim
look too.

He said nothing.

At the West Tower slab, morning had begun above them as a grey thinning of the
air. Darvag and Shandra waited in the basement with blankets, water, and two
lamps. Shandra saw the covered body and put one hand to her mouth. Rorin turned
away, then forced himself back to the stretcher.

"Master Igan?" Darvag asked.

Keria nodded.

The old man took the front of the stretcher without being asked.

They carried Igan through the yard while the lighthouse stayed dark. Grim and
the guards came after, bound and stumbling. Aravele carried the sack of relics.
Keria carried the journal, the book, the brass plates, and the knife wrapped in
cloth. Her shoulder had stiffened until each step found it.

At the quay, Karst looked at the procession and made no joke about weight.

The return crossing was slower. The bay had gone flat under a pale sky. Phlan
waited across the water with smoke rising from breakfast fires and warehouse
chimneys. Men on the docks had already seen the ferry. By the time Karst put in,
half the quay had stopped work.

Hurn came down from the Black Fist post with two guards who looked younger than
their cudgels. Liela arrived soon after, walking quickly, hair pinned hard
against the wind.

"Where is Igan?" she asked.

Keria showed her.

Liela stopped one pace short. No cry, no collapse. She kept both hands at her
sides and made herself look.

Hurn looked at Grim. "Sergeant Thornbuck."

"Sir," Grim said.

It was a stupid word. It was the only one he had left.

Keria laid the evidence on a dock crate one piece at a time. Igan's journal.
Moonsea Cults. The brass plates. The wrapped knife. The relic sack. Then she
told it in order and did not dress it.

Liela wrote. Hurn listened. Aravele corrected two times. The guard who had
spoken in the reliquary spoke again on the dock while the quay watched.

When he finished, Grim had no one left to command.

Hurn ordered the prisoners taken inside. Liela placed her seal on Keria's
written account and kept one copy for House Sokol. Hurn kept one for the Black
Fist. Aravele folded the third and put it inside her coat.

Only then did someone shout from the far end of the quay.

Keria turned.

Across the bay, on Thorn Island, light burned in the tower.

It began as a small gold point above the morning water. Then the mirrors caught
and widened it. The beacon steadied, bright even in daylight, and threw a long
line across the bay toward the docks.

Men took off caps. Someone swore under his breath and then stopped.

Keria thought of the hidden shrine, the brass scales, the body lifted from the
altar, and the cold that had followed them until Grim's hands were tied. She
did not know whether the dead forgave. She doubted they had been asked to.

Karst stood beside her with his cap in his hands.

"That'll bring the boats out," he said.

"Yes."

"And the stories."

"Yes."

The light held. The harbour began to move again. Men untied lines. A cart
rolled over stone. Somewhere behind her, Liela was already giving orders and
Hurn was already complaining about paperwork.

Keria folded Igan's account once, put it inside her coat, and went to find a
priest who knew how to receive a body from the sea wind.
