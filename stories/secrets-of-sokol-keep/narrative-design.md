# Narrative Design

This is a core story-engine artifact for `Secrets of Sokol Keep`.

## Narrative anchor

A darkened beacon forces a working mercenary to follow practical evidence into
a haunted keep, where justice depends on recovering a murdered young man's body
and refusing the convenience of a false account.

## POV and lens

Primary lens: Keria, a human mercenary with enough attachment to Grim to resist
easy judgment and enough discipline to keep looking. Aravele acts as companion,
counterweight, and witness. The prose should stay close to Keria's tasks and
observations: weather, rooms, hands, locks, faces, lies under pressure.

## Dramatic question

Why has the beacon gone dark, and what really happened to Igan Sokol and the
Black Fist detail at the keep?

## Thematic question

When law, loyalty, property, and the dead make competing claims, what form of
justice can the living actually carry back into daylight?

## Ending logic

The ending works only if Grim's guilt is established through evidence rather
than intuition, Harae's demand remains moral rather than sentimental, and Keria
chooses custody and testimony over private revenge. The beacon relighting should
feel like a consequence, not a reward spell.

## Reveal structure and clue ladder

1. Public fact: the beacon has been dark for two days.
2. Social pressure: House Sokol and the Black Fist are shifting blame.
3. Personal thread: Grim was in debt and spoke of treasure below the keep.
4. Island absence: the garrison is gone, but the servants remain frightened and
   alive.
5. Haunting as witness: Harae guides attention toward Igan's rooms and shrine.
6. Documentary key: Igan's journal and `Moonsea Cults` point to the West Tower.
7. Physical entry: fresh earth, cut rope, and the vanishing slab prove a descent
   below the keep.
8. Body evidence: Igan was stabbed in the back at the idol.
9. Motive evidence: Grim and the guards looted the reliquary.
10. Moral resolution: Grim is taken back under guard, and the beacon burns.

## Drift risks and handoff notes

- Do not let the Jade Temple become a generic combat dungeon.
- Keep Harae restrained: witness, pressure, grief, and justice.
- Preserve Keria's discomfort with judging Grim too early.
- Avoid over-defining the old cult; ambiguity is stronger than lore.
- Let lower-status witnesses matter: Imizael, Markoth, Karst, Darvag, Shandra,
  and Rorin all hold pieces of the truth.
