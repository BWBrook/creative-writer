The piece opens in the departure lounge at O’Neill-1, but its first true work
is retrospective. The narrator frames the trip as a California-Moon-L5 circuit
that has already become interpretable in familiar human terms: departure board,
hotel drum, packet ship, itinerary, price bracket, and district. The essential
established fact is that by 2052 space travel is still extraordinary, but it
is no longer unintelligible.

Pacific Meridian Terminal, north of Vandenberg, is described as part clinic,
part airport, part customs hall, and part theatre. The launch vehicle,
*Mojave Clipper*, is fully reusable and handled with the brisk competence once
reserved for ferries and airlines. The launch remains physically violent, but
the surrounding system has become procedural and legible.

After orbit insertion, the narrator stages through Concord Gateway, where robot
tenders, propellant depots, and autonomous welders make the post-2035
automation surge visible. The onward ship, *Selene Packet*, offers gentle
fractional gravity and humane passenger accommodation. This section establishes
that machine industry in orbit quietly ended the age of one-off heroics.
