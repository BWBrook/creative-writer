Moonbase-3, known as Shephard, is presented as severe, competent, and subtly
beautiful. The polar light is slantwise, shadows are absolute, and the Earth
sits low rather than theatrically overhead. The landing is performed by a
machine stack with more patience than a human nervous system, reinforcing the
setting’s deep dependence on automation and procedure.

Shephard’s core identity is that it does not pretend not to be infrastructure.
Its concourse shows pumps, heat exchangers, air handlers, and water systems as
part of the lived environment rather than as hidden backstage machinery. The
Guest Ring offers clean, compact accommodation, while the broader base supports
rim walks, rover excursions, low-gravity sport, a settlement museum, and a
greenhouse promenade.

Most importantly, this chapter establishes lunar water as the basis of the
cislunar economy. Extraction zones, freight lines, and cryogenic handling
plants sit behind every cup of coffee beyond Earth. By the close of the section,
the Moon is no longer just sublime; it has become a place where schoolchildren,
maintenance crews, dentists, and shift workers make the impossible feel routine.
