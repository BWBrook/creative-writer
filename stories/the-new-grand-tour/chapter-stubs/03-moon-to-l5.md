The transition away from Shephard is deliberately anti-romantic. Freight leaves
by electromagnetic rail, passengers leave by hopper, and the cislunar economy
is described as a linked industrial loop rather than a string of isolated
miracles. From lunar orbit, the narrator boards the *Archimedes*, whose
high-thrust transfer drive rejects old science-fiction expectations of a
graceful, languid cruise.

This chapter’s crucial continuity work lies in geometry and sequence. Earth and
the Moon separate into the clean triangular logic of the Earth-Moon L5 point,
and the trip becomes evidence that the California launch system, the lunar ice
economy, and the rotating city all belong to one emerging civilisational stack.

Late in the transfer, O’Neill-1 resolves from astronomy into architecture. It
does not gleam like fantasy; it appears as a dark, shielded, heavily worked
piece of industrial intention. That visual correction is important for every
future story in this setting.
