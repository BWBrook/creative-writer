Arrival begins at the hub, where customs kiosks, freight handlers, and floating
luggage create a working-city chaos that polished renderings never fully admit.
The spoke train to Rim Station gradually restores weight, teaching the body how
to inhabit the station instead of merely visit it.

The narrator then records the settlement’s defining facts: O’Neill-1 is about
eight kilometres long, half a kilometre in radius, and spun slowly enough that
most visitors adjust by breakfast. The city does not pretend to be countryside.
Its avenues curve overhead, the far side of the cylinder hangs upside down in
the sky, and the engineered day arrives through mirror-fed light slots rather
than faux-natural pastoralism.

Population matters here too. At just over forty-two thousand residents plus
transients, O’Neill-1 has crossed the threshold from outpost to city. It can
support anonymity, districts, variation, and the beginnings of ordinary urban
self-consciousness.
