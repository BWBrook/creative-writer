# Narrative Design

## Narrative anchor

The story is a first-person travelogue that demonstrates a social threshold:
by 2052, the California-Moon-L5 corridor has become not just technically
possible, but narratively legible as lived travel.

Its governing move is to let the reader arrive at a civilisational conclusion
through observed infrastructure, hospitality, labour, and ordinary urban life.

## POV and lens

- Primary viewpoint: first-person journalist narrator.
- Narrative distance: observant, literate, and open to wonder, but more
  interested in systems becoming ordinary than in spectacle alone.
- What this lens lets the story notice: how industry, travel, and settlement
  become comprehensible in human-scale terms.

## Dramatic question

Has cislunar expansion crossed the threshold from daring demonstration into a
durable world that people can inhabit, visit, and return to?

## Thematic question

When does frontier become society, and what signs prove that transformation
more honestly than rhetoric or grand visions do?

## Ending logic

- What must be resolved: the story's claim that O'Neill-1 is a real city rather
  than a symbolic outpost or luxury spectacle.
- What should remain open: the narrator's longer return stay and the wider
  horizon of solar-industry expansion.
- What final note or image should the story land on: departure that feels less
  like escape from a marvel than leaving a place that has already become real.

## Reveal structure and clue ladder

1. First signal: launch and transfer are still intense, but already governed by
   recognisable travel logic.
2. Escalation: the Moon reveals infrastructure, extraction, and ordinary civic
   life rather than only sublime remoteness.
3. Reframe or reversal: the move to L5 turns the itinerary into evidence of a
   coherent cislunar system.
4. Final reveal boundary: O'Neill-1 proves itself as a city, while the sequel
   horizon remains open toward longer residence and farther destinations.

## Drift risks and handoff notes

- Preserve the premium-but-not-casual status of cislunar travel.
- Keep labour and infrastructure visible beneath luxury and tourism.
- Avoid treating O'Neill-1 as pastoral fantasy; it should remain compact,
  urban, and engineered.
- Sequel work can deepen residence, governance, and industry, but should keep
  this story's core thesis intact: ordinary life is the true marker of arrival.
