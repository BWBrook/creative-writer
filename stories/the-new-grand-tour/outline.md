# Outline

## Premise

An unnamed journalist documents a California-Moon-L5 travel circuit in 2052,
showing that cislunar tourism has become legible, inhabitable, and socially
real. The piece begins as a travel account and gradually reveals a wider claim:
the Moon and O’Neill-1 are no longer stunts or outposts, but places where
ordinary life, industry, and culture have already taken root.

## Narrative arc

1. California departure and orbital transfer establish that launch has become
   structured travel rather than heroic exception.
2. Shephard on the Moon shows industrial competence, lunar water economics, and
   the early ordinary life of settlement.
3. The transfer to L5 reframes the journey as part of a larger cislunar system.
4. O’Neill-1 is revealed not as spectacle alone, but as the first convincing
   city beyond Earth.
5. The narrator concludes that this whole sequence is only an early stage of a
   larger outward expansion.

## Continuity anchors

- The year is 2052.
- Cislunar tourism exists, but remains expensive enough to feel premium rather
  than casual mass transit.
- The post-2035 automation surge is a foundational enabling fact in orbit and
  beyond.
- Lunar south-polar ice extraction underwrites much of the cislunar economy.
- O’Neill-1 at Earth-Moon L5 is a compact, functioning city of about forty-two
  thousand residents plus transients.
- The narrator leaves already thinking about a longer return stay.

## Sequel vectors

- A longer journalistic stay at Shephard, focusing on work rather than tourism.
- A return to O’Neill-1 long enough to follow neighbourhood life, governance,
  industry, and social change.
- A new itinerary extending onward to an early Mars colony.
- A future story tracing the first practical stages of solar-industry expansion
  beyond the Earth-Moon system.
