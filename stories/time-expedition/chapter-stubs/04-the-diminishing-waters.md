## Chapter purpose

Complete the evidentiary ladder from physical anomaly to ecological collapse,
then place the Traveller at the final station with an informed reason to stay
and a visibly shrinking retreat margin.

## POV and pressure

The Traveller remains the reported first-person witness. His confidence grows
because different lines of evidence converge, while exhaustion and the limits
of one locality make that confidence increasingly dangerous.

## Required movement

- Advance to roughly twenty-three months after baseline and repeat the survey.
- Add aquatic distress, vegetation failure, prey movement, and reduced animal
  abundance.
- Reintroduce the scarred predator as watchful, leaner, and still cautious.
- Test local weather, season, and water explanations before widening the
  hypothesis to powerful distant volcanic action.
- Advance to roughly thirty months after baseline.
- Detect early seepage or stone movement in the refuge.
- Fix the proof satchel to the machine and elect to observe for three days.

## Continuity notes

- Avoid a list of disasters; each observation must revise the working model.
- Chapter III leaves him pleased with an imagined upstream diversion. He has
  neither found the new course nor traced the assumed river–lake connection.
  Test this local explanation against the widening changes before replacing it;
  it does not establish the later flood's actual course.
- At Station III, falling grains precede any displaced indicator: Chapter II
  explicitly records the chalk line and plumb as unaltered.
- The predator's identity remains probable rather than certain.
- Fire and tremors remain absent unless later physical verification makes one
  indispensable.
- The Traveller still believes the fissure refuge offers a defensible base.
