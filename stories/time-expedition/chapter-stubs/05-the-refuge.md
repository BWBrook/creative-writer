## Chapter purpose

Turn the established environmental sequence into immediate survival pressure
and make escape depend upon the same refuge, animal behaviour, evidence, and
machine constraints developed through the investigation.

## POV and pressure

The Traveller's reported account remains concrete and sequential. His problem
is access to the machine under changing water, unstable stone, and an animal
whose desperation has overcome its earlier caution.

## Required movement

- Continue observation through the final occupied days without adding a new
  catalogue of mechanisms.
- Let extreme runoff alter the river's course and undermine the ridge.
- Drive displaced animals to high ground and the predator towards the refuge.
- Use the revolver as a brief deterrent without a convenient misfire or kill.
- Obstruct the machine through debris and progressive roof fall.
- Force a choice between the full archive and immediate access to the controls.
- Preserve only the attached proof satchel, notebook, and instrument register.
- Escape at the point of structural failure.
- Restore the machine's forty-yard displacement physically during a brief
  homeward stop before the laboratory was built.

## Continuity notes

- The predator remains hungry, stressed, and physically dangerous rather than
  malicious or preternaturally intelligent.
- The hydrological event must follow the prior drought and landscape geometry.
- The machine works once physically reached; machinery failure is not the
  climax.
- Final photographs and most sequential samples are lost.
- The revolver returns in its case beneath his coat, as Chapter II establishes.
- The brief Richmond stop permits the final hauling and shirt-strip bandages;
  it adds no further ancient observation station or lateral machine power.
