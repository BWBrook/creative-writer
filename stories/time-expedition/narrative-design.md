# Narrative Design

## Narrative anchor

*The Last Week of the Permian* is the first standalone expedition in a
recovered anthology of further journeys by Wells's Time Traveller. The
anonymous narrator records two evenings in the Richmond study and a special
Royal Society meeting. Inside that frame, Filby reads an interrupted expedition
notebook and the Traveller delivers the principal first-person account.

The story uses a five-act concentric-testimony structure across six numbered
chapters and an epilogue. It targets 18,000 to 22,000 words, with room to reach
24,000 only where scene pressure requires it.

## POV and lens

- The anonymous narrator governs the outer study scenes, Filby's notebook
  reading, the public address, the examination, and the epilogue.
- Filby remains an active interlocutor. He provokes the expedition, presses for
  the notebook account, arranges the meeting, and tests the Traveller in
  private.
- Notebook passages are compressed, procedural, and increasingly incomplete.
  They provide observations without supplying the crisis narrative twice.
- The Traveller's Royal Society account becomes immersive reported first
  person once it passes the notebook's last complete entry.
- The Society examination restores social distance and separates observation,
  sequence, probability, and conjecture.
- No ancient scene receives modern omniscient confirmation. Modern Permian
  science remains backstage guidance for coherent selection.

## Dramatic question

Can the Time Traveller understand enough of the mounting local catastrophe to
recover the machine and escape when the refuge that made his investigation
possible begins to fail?

## Thematic question

How much can disciplined science establish from one locality, incomplete
instruments, damaged evidence, and a witness whose power to revisit the scene
still leaves him vulnerable to error?

## Ending logic

- The immediate expedition resolves when the Traveller recovers the machine
  from the failing refuge and returns alive.
- The temporal sequence establishes a real local collapse, while its global
  extent and ultimate cause remain contestable.
- The Society accepts the journey and physical evidence provisionally. The
  Geologist withholds assent from the Traveller's broadest interpretation.
- The Traveller returns chastened about complete explanation and more strongly
  drawn to temporal exploration.
- The epilogue lands on altered calculations or machine settings. It implies
  preparation without naming the next destination.
- The temporal obstruction surrounding his earlier return remains an
  anthology-level question.

## Reveal structure and clue ladder

1. The Traveller explains that a temporal obstruction delayed his return after
   the second departure recorded in *The Time Machine*.
2. Filby's account of an unresolved Permian faunal termination supplies a
   scientific target rather than a precise date.
3. The notebook establishes ancient Richmond, the fissure refuge, a baseline
   survey, the complete fish specimen, and a large predator that ignores the
   Traveller.
4. The first forward interval brings persistent haze, abnormal heat, and
   measurable changes in river and lake margins.
5. The second interval adds aquatic distress, vegetation failure, prey
   movement, and renewed signs of the same scarred predator.
6. A final advance establishes directional collapse rather than ordinary
   seasonal variation. The Traveller remains for roughly three continuous
   days to observe the crisis.
7. Floodwater changes the river's course, undermines the refuge, and drives the
   starving predator towards risks it previously avoided.
8. The Traveller's revolver buys moments but cannot solve a confined encounter
   beside an unstable machine. He abandons most of the evidence to escape.
9. The surviving notebook, fish, paired samples, and limited photographs
   establish a serious case without proving a planetary cause.
10. The examination exposes the unresolved terrestrial anchoring problem and
    forces the Traveller to reduce his confidence in distant volcanic action.

## Architecture commitments

### Temporal sampling

The Traveller occupies four stations in one forward sequence: baseline, about
thirteen months later, about twenty-three months after baseline, and a final
station about thirty months after baseline. These are approximate machine
calibrations rather than geological dates. He spends about two days at the
baseline station, where the machine must be moved and the marks established,
roughly one day at each of the two middle stations, and about three days at
the last: some seven days of his own accumulated experience, which is what the
title describes, rather than a seven-day global extinction claim.

### Predator continuity

The large theriodont-like predator bears a conspicuous scar across the left
shoulder. The Traveller treats later sightings as one animal; the account
supplies enough similarity to make that reasonable without proving identity.
The Comparative Anatomist may accept the behavioural inference while noting
that no specimen or exact identification supports it.

### Photography

The Traveller carries a compact period hand camera. A few early exposures
survive and can be developed before the Society meeting. They establish the
landscape and perhaps the predator's scale, but their distance and quality
limit interpretation. The final and potentially decisive exposures are lost
or spoiled during the refuge failure. Exact camera and film handling require
just-in-time period verification before drafting.

### Firearm

Having regretted his lack of arms during the first journey, the Traveller
carries a substantial period revolver. Its report and one grazing hit can turn
an early rush. Limited ammunition, the animal's size, the confined refuge, and
the risk of firing towards the machine make it a delaying tool rather than a
solution. It does not fail conveniently. The exact model and handling require
period verification before drafting.

### Surviving exhibits

Before the final observations, the Traveller fixes a compact proof satchel to
the machine. It preserves the fish in spirit; baseline and Station III air
tubes; baseline and Station III water bottles; one Station III deposit packet;
and one early exposed film roll. The notebook and instrument register remain
on his person. The sequential plant pressings, most sediment samples, bulky
instruments, and final photographs are lost. Contamination and incomplete
provenance remain available to the Society questioners.

## Drift risks and handoff notes

- Wellsian authorship authenticity outranks modern scientific perfection.
- The title refers to the Traveller's experienced week, not a literal global
  extinction completed in seven days.
- Keep the catastrophe cumulative and legible. Avoid a catalogue of every
  proposed end-Permian mechanism.
- The predator is an animal under ecological pressure. It never becomes a
  calculating antagonist.
- Use late-1890s terms in character speech. Modern dates, clades, tectonics,
  and extinction mechanisms belong only in backstage planning.
- Preserve local uncertainty. The Traveller may infer distant volcanic action
  but cannot locate Siberia or establish global synchrony.
- Let notebook compression and Society disagreement carry information that
  would otherwise become explanatory narration.
- Each substantial examination question must alter confidence, reveal a limit,
  or add a fact.
- Source-check exact instruments, firearm, camera, preservatives, meeting
  procedure, and specialist vocabulary only when drafting reaches them.
- Do not name the next expedition or resolve the temporal obstruction here.
