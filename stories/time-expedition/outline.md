# Outline

## Anthology premise

*Time Expedition* is a recovered anthology of further journeys by H. G.
Wells's Time Traveller. Each expedition stands alone and remains shorter than
*The Time Machine*. A framing spine may emerge later, but this story needs no
announced sequel or shared narrative arc.

## Expedition I synopsis

After the second departure recorded in *The Time Machine*, the Traveller finds
that a temporal obstruction prevents his intended return. He reaches Richmond
later than he meant to and eventually completes the account for Filby and the
anonymous narrator. Filby's description of a lecture on the unexplained end of
Permian life turns the conversation towards the machine's untested backward
range. Scientific curiosity, vanity, and appetite for adventure lead the
Traveller to prepare a compact field expedition.

He returns from that expedition in collapse. During his convalescence, Filby
reads the recovered notebook aloud. It records the uncertain descent into deep
time, the effort of dragging the machine into a fissure refuge, baseline
surveys of an arid tropical landscape, collection of a complete ganoid fish,
and an encounter with a large scarred predator that ignores him. Later entries
become terse and disconnected. The restored Traveller interrupts the reading
and insists upon a public account before qualified men.

At a special Royal Society meeting, he resumes where the notebook fails. Four
forward stations across roughly thirty months show haze, heat, shrinking
waters, biological distress, faunal displacement, and the scarred predator's
change from indifference to desperate watchfulness. He interprets the sequence
as a local expression of a wider catastrophe, perhaps driven by distant
volcanic action. During three continuous days at the final station, extreme
runoff changes the river, undermines the refuge, and drives the starving animal
inside. A revolver delays it but cannot secure the confined space. The
Traveller clears the machine and escapes while most of his evidence is lost.

The Society accepts the journey more readily than the global explanation. A
Geologist, Chemist, Comparative Anatomist, and Astronomer test the dating,
samples, specimen, predator inference, and unexplained terrestrial anchoring
of the machine. The Traveller distinguishes what he saw from what he believes.
Later, over cigars and smoking bishop, Filby finds him sobered by uncertainty
and already preparing another calculation.

## Chapter order

### I. The Obstruction

The anonymous narrator joins the Traveller and Filby in the Richmond study as
the Traveller completes the account of his delayed return. The temporal
obstruction is reported through observed effects rather than solved. Filby
introduces the Permian mystery; conversation reveals the Traveller's mixed
motives. A compact expedition is prepared, including a hand camera, specimen
gear, instruments, and a revolver. The chapter ends with backward departure.

### II. The Note-book

The Traveller returns injured and exhausted, then convalesces for more than a
week. Filby reads the notebook to the narrator. Its orderly entries cover
arrival, revival, relocation of the machine to a fissure refuge, baseline
surveys, the fish specimen, and the indifferent scarred predator. Its later
entries record isolated irregularities and then break down. The Traveller
interrupts and asks Filby to arrange a special Royal Society meeting.

### III. The First Intervals

At Burlington House, the Traveller authenticates the chain of testimony and
passes quickly over notebook material. His immersive account begins with the
first forward interval, about thirteen months after baseline. Persistent haze,
heat, river recession, and altered lake margins remain individually
explainable. Their recurrence in the same measured places begins a directional
case. Temporal sampling already exacts fatigue and calibration uncertainty.

### IV. The Diminishing Waters

At the second and third forward stations, aquatic distress, vegetation failure,
prey movement, and the scarred predator's watchfulness converge. The Traveller
tests rival local explanations and finds each too narrow. His final advance
places him about thirty months beyond baseline. He elects to remain for three
days, fixes the proof satchel to the machine, and begins continuous observation
with a shrinking retreat margin.

### V. The Refuge

Heat and water stress yield to extreme runoff. The river changes course across
the plain, undercuts the ridge, and drives displaced animals towards high
ground. The predator enters the refuge in desperation. The Traveller's
revolver creates space without ending the threat. Roof fall and debris block
the machine. He abandons most specimens and instruments, clears the controls,
and departs as the refuge fails.

### VI. The Examination

A gadfly challenges the premise; prior witnesses and the surviving evidence
establish a serious case. The Geologist tests date, locality, and global
inference. The Chemist exposes sample limitations. The Comparative Anatomist
examines the fish and questions the predator identification. The Astronomer
raises the unexplained terrestrial anchoring of the machine. The Traveller
reduces several claims while defending the observed sequence. Acceptance is
substantial and divided.

### Epilogue. Smoking Bishop

Back in Richmond, Filby and the Traveller consider what the public meeting
could not settle. The Traveller admits the limits of local testimony without
losing his appetite for inquiry. Filby notices altered calculations or a small
change at the machine. The destination and the temporal obstruction remain
unnamed.

## Architecture boundary

The detailed scene sequence lives under `scene-cards/`. This outline fixes the
story order and evidence ladder but does not supply draft prose. Exact period
equipment, terminology, and scientific phrasing receive just-in-time source
checks during drafting rather than another broad research phase.
