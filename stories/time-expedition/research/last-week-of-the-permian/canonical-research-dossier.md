# Purpose

This document distils the accepted research foundation for *The Last Week of
the Permian*. It is shorter and more directive than the external Pro report.
It records the scientific reconstruction available backstage, the knowledge
and language available in the late 1890s, and the fictional choices permitted
for the next architecture pass.

The [approved story design](story-design-2026-07-26.md)
remains authoritative for structure, character roles, temporal rules, and
ending. This dossier does not draft prose or change the five-act design.

The governing priority remains:

1. Wellsian authorship authenticity.
2. Late-1890s epistemic authenticity.
3. Internal scientific coherence.
4. Modern scientific plausibility.

Modern science supplies the hidden causal model. It does not give Wells or the
Traveller modern omniscience.

# Status Model

Every important decision has two independent statuses.

## Evidence status

- **Established:** supported by strong convergent evidence.
- **Supported:** a responsible interpretation with meaningful limits.
- **Contested:** credible alternatives remain.
- **Speculative:** direct evidence is absent or too weak for confidence.
- **Unsuitable:** materially conflicts with evidence or period authenticity.

## Story-choice status

- **Adopted:** use in detailed architecture unless a material conflict emerges.
- **Provisional:** use as a working assumption and resolve during architecture.
- **Reserve:** available only if scene logic demonstrates a real need.
- **Rejected:** do not use.

An adopted story choice need not be established science. It must instead be
plausible, clearly bounded, and honest about its evidentiary status.

# Modern Backstage Model

## Place and climate

The crustal tract now beneath Richmond lay on the exposed London-Brabant high.
The British Geological Survey describes the London region during the Permian
as probably a rocky desert and records no preserved Permian deposits there.
This establishes broad aridity and exposure, but erases local evidence for
topography, drainage, water bodies, caves, plants, and animals.

A direct Paleolatitude.org 3.0 calculation for modern Richmond coordinates at
251.9 Ma returned a mean palaeolatitude of 17.34 degrees N, with model bounds
of 15.46 to 19.30 degrees N. This supports a hot, strongly seasonal northern
tropical setting. It does not supply longitude, elevation, river direction, or
distance to a shoreline.

For the story, the high overlooks an alluvial plain crossed by a substantial
seasonal river. Water terminates in a saline lake, chain of persistent pools,
or playa. Patchy conifer and seed-fern scrub follows better-watered ground;
moisture-loving plants occupy channel margins. Bare weathered rock, red
sediment, salt crust, scrub, and concentrated water provide a varied setting
without inventing a nearby open sea.

Sources:

- [BGS, London and the Thames Valley](https://webapps.bgs.ac.uk/memoirs/docs/B01360.html)
- [BGS, Southern North Sea geology](https://webapps.bgs.ac.uk/memoirs/docs/B01848.html)
- [Paleolatitude.org 3.0](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0346817)

## Timing and mechanism

The formal Permian-Triassic boundary is a stratigraphic datum, not the instant
at which all Permian life vanished. The main high-resolution marine extinction
interval is measured in tens of thousands of years. Terrestrial disturbances
were regionally asynchronous and may precede or follow particular marine
records.

The modern causal backbone is Siberian Traps magmatism, large carbon release,
greenhouse warming, carbon-cycle disruption, and severe marine oxygen stress.
On land, warming and hydrological instability can drive vegetation stress,
erosion, changing runoff, and faunal displacement. Ozone damage, widespread
toxic metals, universal wildfire, and atmospheric hydrogen sulphide remain
contested or unsuitable as required story mechanisms.

The Traveller witnesses a selected local episode within this prolonged and
uneven crisis. His time machine lets him detect direction by comparing one
place at different times. That mechanism makes the fictional compression
legible without converting a multi-millennial event into a literal global
week.

Sources:

- [Burgess, Bowring and Shen 2014](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1317692111)
- [Burgess and Bowring 2015](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.1500470)
- [Dal Corso et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43017-021-00259-4)
- [Wu et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10830061/)
- [Chu et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-37717-0)

## Adopted clue ladder

The next architecture pass should build from repeated variables, not introduce
a new disaster at every stop.

| Stage | Directly available evidence | Hidden modern interpretation | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | Seasonal river, saline terminal water, patchy scrub, ordinary fauna; fish collected; predator indifferent | Late-Permian arid low-latitude community | Exact community is fictional reconstruction |
| Recurrent heat | Higher daily maxima, warmer nights, haze, altered pressure and winds | Sustained climatic forcing | Individual observations may still resemble weather |
| Water stress | Retreating margins, warmer concentrated pools, salt crust, turbid water | Drought, evaporation and chemical concentration | No global ocean inference |
| Aquatic distress | Crowding, rapid gill movement, sluggish fish, deaths, dark mud, decay odour | Combined heat, oxygen shortage, salinity and local chemistry | Traveller cannot isolate one cause in the field |
| Vegetation failure | Wilting, shedding, root exposure, reduced insect activity, bare ground | Heat and water stress with erosion feedback | Disease and season remain alternatives early |
| Faunal displacement | Movement towards water, scavenging, prey scarcity, unfamiliar risk-taking | Habitat and food failure | Migration is not proof of extinction |
| Hydrological reversal | Pressure fall, violent rain, sheet flow, channel avulsion, debris | Extreme runoff over damaged catchment | Local storm cannot prove planetary synchrony |
| Continuous climax | Predator approaches refuge; flood scours entrance; cracks enlarge; roof block rotates and falls | Ordinary animal desperation plus progressive shelter failure | Immediate danger is local even if its preconditions are widespread |

No early observation should prove the final explanation. Heat, haze, water,
plants, animals, and ground movement become persuasive only as a sequence.

# Local Life

## Community boundary

No terminal-Permian fauna is known from Richmond. The architecture may use
guilds and broad body plans supported by low-latitude and north-west European
analogues, but must not transplant the Karoo or Russia wholesale.

Permitted background forms include:

- open coniferous and seed-fern scrub;
- equisetaceous or other moisture-dependent channel vegetation;
- insects, arachnids, and small carrion feeders;
- temporary-water branchiopods and ostracods;
- small ganoid fishes in persistent reaches;
- small reptiles and mammal-like forms among rocks and scrub;
- sparse beaked or heavy herbivorous reptiles; and
- an unnamed terrestrial carnivore described before it is classified.

Avoid *Glossopteris*, *Lystrosaurus*, *Inostrancevia*, or *Scutosaurus* as
local scenery merely because they are familiar Permian icons.

Source:

- [Bernardi et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.earscirev.2017.10.002)

## Complete specimen

The adopted specimen is an undamaged fish approximately 12-20 cm long,
collected during the baseline phase rather than during the die-off. It has
rhomboidal scales, a heterocercal tail, distinct fins, gill structures, and
small teeth. The Traveller may compare it with a ganoid fish; the later
Comparative Anatomist may call it *Palaeoniscus*-like or compare it with
*Acrolepis* while refusing species identification.

The fish is preserved in a thick, wide-mouthed jar of strong spirit, securely
stoppered and packed. Spirit alters colour and soft tissue, giving the
Comparative Anatomist a legitimate reason to qualify conclusions. Formalin is
available at the edge of the period but is not adopted.

Source:

- [JNCC, Fossil Fishes of Great Britain](https://data.jncc.gov.uk/data/f722dcd3-4cf1-46d8-9624-56abff537c8d/gcr-v16-fossil-fishes-of-great-britain-c10.pdf)

## Predator

The working predator is a lean quadruped roughly 1.3-1.8 m long, with a long
skull, differentiated teeth, and enlarged canine-like teeth. The Traveller can
describe tracks, gait, posture, feeding traces, and visible dentition. These
observations support a carnivorous interpretation but not a precise genus.

In the notebook it should remain unnamed or "theriodont-like". In the lecture
the Traveller may call it a theriodont. The Comparative Anatomist should reduce
that confidence to resemblance with known theriodont forms.

The animal initially disregards the Traveller. Heat, thirst, prey loss,
injury, carrion, shade, or refuge later lowers its normal avoidance threshold.
It does not stalk him for dramatic reasons or behave with human malice. If the
story treats repeated sightings as one individual, the Traveller needs a
visible mark or recurring track pattern, and the Society should still question
the identification.

This remains the largest palaeontological liberty in the adopted setting. The
architecture may replace the body plan if a better low-latitude analogue
emerges, but it may not solve uncertainty by importing a famous named taxon.

# Refuge and Climax

The refuge is a shallow weathered cleft, joint, fault recess, undercut bed, or
talus-roofed shelter in resistant older rock. A slab or block roofs weaker,
jointed, or rubbly material. It appears stable during the baseline phase and
sits above ordinary channel flow.

The Traveller establishes fixed indicators: water-level marks outside, a
plumb line or chalk marks across cracks, a thermometer and barometer station,
sample shelves, and a clear control side for the machine.

The adopted failure chain is:

1. Heat and water stress reduce vegetation cover.
2. Bare ground and damaged channels increase sediment mobility.
3. Exceptional rain produces sheet flow and channel avulsion.
4. Water and debris scour or saturate the shelter's weaker support.
5. Cracks, trickling grains, shifted marks, and small falls precede failure.
6. A slab rotates or drops, narrowing access and wedging debris around the
   machine.
7. The same flood and search for shelter, water, or carrion bring the predator
   towards the fissure.
8. The Traveller clears the controls, abandons bulky evidence, and accepts the
   danger of rematerialisation because remaining is worse.

The collapse must not conveniently kill the predator, expose magma, or become
a perfectly timed total cave-in. A tremor is not part of the adopted chain.

# Late-1890s Scientific Layer

## Available knowledge

By the late 1890s, a scientifically literate Briton could know that:

- Murchison named the Permian System in 1841;
- it lay between the Carboniferous and Trias;
- red beds, magnesian limestone, salt deposits, and sparse or distinctive
  fossils characterised important British and European occurrences;
- major changes in fossil faunas occurred between geological systems;
- ordinary natural processes could produce exceptional catastrophe;
- gases absorbed and emitted heat, through Tyndall's work;
- Arrhenius had recently connected atmospheric carbonic acid with temperature;
- anomodont, theriodont, labyrinthodont, *Gorgonops*, *Lycosaurus*,
  *Palaeoniscus*, and *Acrolepis* were available names or comparisons; and
- Wells himself used "Permo-triassic", "reptile-like mammals",
  "mammal-like reptiles", "conditions of life", "living equilibrium", and
  "extinction".

They could not know plate tectonics, Pangaea, radiometric dates, the Siberian
Traps extinction model, isotope excursions, global carbon budgets, or modern
phylogenetic classifications.

Relevant period sources:

- [Ramsay, *The Physical Geology and Geography of Great Britain*, 1878](https://www.geology.19thcenturyscience.org/books/1878-Ramsay-Geology/htm/doc.html)
- [Owen, *Catalogue of the Fossil Reptilia of South Africa*, 1876](https://books.google.com/books?id=FPJeaTdtACMC)
- [Lydekker, British Museum fossil reptile catalogue, 1890](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/item/125712)
- [Tyndall 1861](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstl.1861.0001)
- [Arrhenius 1896](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786449608620846)
- [Wells, *Text-Book of Biology*](../../original/text/text-book-of-biology-part-1.md)

## Period terminology

| Backstage concept | Permitted period expression | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Permian-Triassic boundary | passage from the Permian to the Trias; Permo-Triassic passage | No numerical age from geology |
| Mass extinction | great extinction; disappearance or termination of a fauna; organic crisis | No modern percentages or assumed synchrony |
| Ecosystem | living association; conditions of life; living equilibrium | Prefer concrete relations to abstract jargon |
| Food-web collapse | failure of prey; relations of food; scarcity of nourishment | Show the chain through observation |
| Climate change | change of climate; secular climatic alteration | Cause remains disputed |

| Greenhouse warming | warming associated with atmospheric carbonic acid | Arrhenius is unusually current, not common knowledge |
| Hydrogen sulphide | sulphuretted hydrogen | Local sample and odour only |

## Expedition capability

The adopted compact capability includes:

- two notebooks, numbered labels, pencils, indelible ink, and a watch;
- an aneroid barometer, shaded maximum/minimum thermometers, and a compact
  wet-and-dry-bulb instrument;
- prismatic compass, pocket clinometer, tape, folding rule, and plumb line;
- pocket lens, compact microscope, slides, tubes, and small balance;
- litmus, limewater, lead-acetate paper, silver nitrate, and a few other simple
  qualitative reagents;
- glass-stoppered water bottles, sealable gas tubes, packets and tins, a small
  plant press, and a wide-mouthed spirit jar;
- fine net, forceps, scalpel, needles, twine, hammer, chisel, and folding spade;
- waterproof matches, candle ends, lantern, bandages, antiseptic, and a boiling
  vessel; and
- a compact camera, subject to the architecture decision below.

Measurements are comparative and qualitative. The Traveller can record trends
in temperature, pressure, water level, salinity by residue or hydrometer,
acidity, simple chemical reactions, plant condition, and animal counts. He
cannot identify isotope changes, make laboratory-grade gas analyses in the
cave, or diagnose a planetary atmosphere from one reagent paper.

Samples at each stop use a repeated series: air, water, sediment, plant,
meteorological readings, fixed-view image if available, and brief animal
counts. Labels carry stop number and dial interval rather than a false calendar
date. Richmond controls travel with the kit.

# Royal Society Examination

The adopted public setting is a special meeting in the Royal Society's rooms
at Burlington House, on a Thursday afternoon, modelled on documented special
and discussion meetings in 1898. An unnamed Fellow arranges or communicates
the account. The President or a Vice-President chairs. Specimens, notebooks,
sample tubes, drawings, and machine components occupy the table. The full
machine remains at Richmond and may be inspected later by a small deputation.

The Traveller gives the substantial first-person account. Questions are
recognised by the chair and identified by occupation:

- **Geologist:** tests dating, local-to-global inference, volcanic evidence,
  migration versus extinction, and measured versus continuous intervals.
- **Astronomer:** tests latitude, dial calibration, and why the machine remains
  with a moving Earth.
- **Comparative Anatomist:** tests the fish, preservation damage, predator
  classification, and whether repeated sightings identify one animal.
- **Chemist or natural philosopher:** tests controls, contaminated samples,
  qualitative reactions, and the leap from local gas or water chemistry to a
  distant volcanic source.

The meeting may provisionally accept temporal travel and genuinely anomalous
material while leaving the exact date and global causal synthesis disputed.
The examination must qualify the Traveller's certainty rather than simply
award him victories.

Historical precedents:

- [Special Royal Society eclipse meeting, 1898](https://doi.org/10.1038/057488a0)
- [Royal Society Antarctic discussion, 1898](https://doi.org/10.1038/057413a0)
- [Royal Society conversazione programme, 1896](https://catalogues.royalsociety.org/CalmView/Record.aspx?id=PC%2F3%2F2%2F14&src=CalmView.Catalog)

# Canonical Machine Constraints

Only [*The Time Machine*](../../original/text/the-time-machine.md) governs the
machine.

- It travels in both temporal directions.
- It remains at the same terrestrial locality while the landscape changes.
- Small and enormous intervals are possible, but extreme calibration accuracy
  is not established.
- High-speed travel causes confusion, swaying, sickness, and exhaustion.
- Stopping where matter occupies the same space risks interpenetration,
  chemical reaction, and explosion.
- The stopped machine can be moved in ordinary space.
- Its small operating levers can be removed and carried.
- It is mechanically exposed to oil, dirt, handling, obstruction, and partial
  dismantling.
- Clothes, dirt, flowers, and other small carried objects can return.
- Canon gives no exact cargo limit or complete theory of what travels with the
  machine.

The story must leave unresolved why the machine follows Earth, how it remains
attached to the same deforming crustal tract, how much empty volume permits a
safe stop, and how its dials behave across approximately 252 million years.
The Astronomer can articulate the problem more sharply than the Traveller can
answer it.

# Anachronism Guardrails

Do not place the following in a character's unqualified vocabulary or
knowledge:

- Pangaea, continental drift, plate tectonics, or a mapped Siberian source;
- a geological age of 251.9 Ma independent of the machine;
- mass-extinction percentages or global synchrony;
- ecosystem, biodiversity, food web, trophic cascade, anoxia, euxinia,
  radiative forcing, or ozone depletion as modern technical packages;
- mercury, nickel, or isotope excursions as observed evidence;
- modern clades and post-1899 taxonomic names;
- a deep local karst cavern, local volcanic vent, or direct Siberian ash and
  earthquakes;
- a calculating monster predator;
- laboratory-grade field chemistry, perfect sample preservation, or modern
  forensic chain-of-custody language;
- modern conference procedure; or
- a public demonstration that turns the meeting into spectacle.

# Architecture Decision Handoff

The research phase reduced the remaining choices to five matters that belong
in detailed architecture rather than the scientific evidence layer:

1. **Temporal sampling:** the working assumption is about three real years and
   a small number of stops. Architecture must set the exact intervals and show
   why they reveal direction without implying modern certainty.
2. **Predator continuity:** decide whether one visibly marked individual
   recurs or whether the Traveller infers identity and is challenged.
3. **Photography:** a compact camera is source-consistent. Decide whether it,
   exposed film, both, or neither survive the refuge failure, and ensure that
   photographs strengthen rather than settle the evidence.
4. **Firearm:** after regretting his lack of arms in the original expedition,
   the Traveller needs an explicit reason either to carry a period firearm or
   omit one. If carried, its limitations must arise naturally rather than from
   convenient failure.
5. **Surviving exhibits:** select the smallest persuasive set from notebook,
   instrument register, fish, air tubes, water bottles, deposit packets,
   pressed plants, and photographs. The lost evidence should have been capable
   of distinguishing rival explanations.

# Research Stop Rule

The broad Permian research phase is complete at the useful-core level. Further
research is justified only when detailed architecture encounters a decisive
question about:

- a specific period term or taxonomic comparison;
- operation of a named Victorian instrument or preservative;
- physical feasibility of a precise refuge incident;
- Royal Society procedure needed by a particular scene; or
- a source-canon constraint in *The Time Machine*.

Do not reopen the whole literature search to polish marginal details. This
dossier is canonical and has been carried into the story's narrative design,
outline, chapter stubs, registries, and scene cards. Manuscript prose remains
outside the research and architecture passes.
