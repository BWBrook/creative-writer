# Chapter I Period and Canon Brief

## Purpose

This is the narrow pre-drafting brief for *The Obstruction*. It records only
facts and language that affect the Richmond frame, Filby's provocation, the
expedition kit, and the backward departure. The wider Permian reconstruction
remains governed by `canonical-research-dossier.md`.

## Source-canon continuity

- The original narrator last saw the Traveller carrying a small camera and a
  knapsack before the second departure. He then waited three years and wrote
  that the Traveller had never returned. Chapter I may therefore correct the
  narrator's published conclusion after a return later than expected; it must
  not imply that the original witnesses knew the conclusion was premature.
- During his first expedition, the Traveller explicitly regretted travelling
  without arms, medicine, tobacco, enough matches, or a Kodak. A camera,
  matches, tobacco, simple medicine, and a revolver are earned corrections to
  an established mistake rather than retrospective embellishments.
- Canon establishes separate controls for forward and backward travel,
  coarse dial readings, severe physical discomfort at speed, danger when
  stopping in occupied matter, and ordinary movement of the stopped machine.
  It does not explain terrestrial anchoring, exact calibration over geological
  time, or a temporal obstruction.
- The second future journey should remain bounded in Chapter I. Its dramatic
  work is to produce the delayed return and the obstruction, not to open a
  second complete adventure before the Permian expedition begins.

Primary source: [H. G. Wells, *The Time Machine*](../../original/text/the-time-machine.md),
especially Chapters VI-XII and the Epilogue.

## Period geological language

Ramsay's 1878 account gives a scientifically literate late-Victorian speaker a
firm but incomplete problem. He places the Permian between the Carboniferous
and the New Red Sandstone or Trias, calls parts of it transitional, notes the
small number and dwarfed aspect of many fossils, and describes the old
Palaeozoic grouping as giving way to a Secondary or Mesozoic character above
the Permian.

Filby may therefore report:

- the Permian System and the succeeding Trias or New Red Sandstone;
- the poverty or dwindling of a fauna;
- old genera disappearing at the passage into the Trias;
- uncertainty over whether the record shows gradual physical change, local
  conditions, migration, imperfect preservation, or a wider termination; and
- a lecturer or discussion at Burlington House without inventing a named,
  verbatim historical lecture.

Avoid `mass extinction`, `biodiversity`, `ecosystem collapse`, a numerical
date, modern percentages, or a settled global cause. The Traveller may infer a
possible catastrophe, but the Victorian evidence does not entitle him to begin
with one.

Primary sources:

- [A. C. Ramsay, "Permian Strata," *The Physical Geology and Geography of Great Britain* (1878)](https://www.geology.19thcenturyscience.org/books/1878-Ramsay-Geology/text-ocr/text/Chap10.html)
- [A. C. Ramsay, "New Red Sandstone and Marl, and Rhaetic Beds," *The Physical Geology and Geography of Great Britain* (1878)](https://www.geology.19thcenturyscience.org/books/1878-Ramsay-Geology/text-ocr/text/Chap11.html)

## Equipment boundary

- Retain Wells's `small camera`; no model name or photographic specification
  is needed in Chapter I.
- Use a `substantial revolver` or simply a `revolver`, carried in a leather
  case. Revolvers and self-cocking actions were ordinary pre-1890 technology,
  but an exact make, calibre, cartridge, and mechanism are deferred until the
  weapon matters in Chapter V.
- Let capacity appear through rejected and retained objects rather than a full
  catalogue. Likely visible choices are the camera, notebooks and pencils,
  aneroid, thermometer case, specimen bottles, spirit jar, matches, tobacco,
  provisions, medicine, and revolver.
- The machine has no canonically stated load limit. The practical constraint is
  restricted space, secure attachment, and the Traveller's ability to move the
  machine and carry the kit after arrival.

Period mechanical witness: [G. H. Harrington and W. A. Richardson, "Revolver," US Patent 360,686 (1887)](https://patents.google.com/patent/US360686A/en).

## Temporal-obstruction boundary

Describe the obstruction through repeated observed effects: the intended
laboratory interval will not settle into view, the controls resist or recoil,
and the Traveller must overshoot the desired return. He may conjecture that
his earliest experiments disturbed the local course of duration, using such
words as `disturbance`, `eddy`, or `inequality`.

Do not use `temporal shell`, `field`, `resonance`, `timeline`, `spacetime`, or
modern causal certainty. The obstruction remains an anthology thread and is
not related to the Permian catastrophe.

## Chapter I drafting decisions

1. Open with the narrator correcting, rather than evading, his earlier report.
2. Restore Filby as blunt, argumentative, and socially recognisable; he is the
   provoker, not a Watson-like recorder.
3. Give only enough of the second expedition to establish the failed return.
4. Let geological uncertainty, scientific vanity, and appetite for adventure
   converge in the decision to go backwards.
5. Make coarse targeting explicit. The Traveller can seek the close of the
   Permian, never a particular day.
6. End with witnessed departure. Defer transit and the Permian landscape to
   Chapter II.
