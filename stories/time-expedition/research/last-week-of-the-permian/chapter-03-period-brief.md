# Chapter III Period and Continuity Brief

This narrow drafting brief supports the Burlington House opening and the first
forward comparison. The canonical research dossier and narrative design remain
the governing references. These checks do not reopen the wider research phase.

## The meeting

A contemporary notice announces a special Royal Society meeting at its
Burlington House rooms on Thursday 31 March 1898, at 4.30 p.m. This supports a
special Thursday-afternoon meeting in the chosen period; the Traveller's
meeting and its arrangements remain fictional.

- [Nature, 24 March 1898, Notes](https://www.nature.com/articles/057488a0)

A March 1898 paper by Edwin Edser is recorded as communicated by Captain
Abney, F.R.S. A Fellow agreeing to communicate a non-Fellow's account therefore
has a contemporary precedent. This does not confer an automatic right to a
special meeting: Filby's letters and the Fellow's visit supply the fictional
arrangement, without implying that sponsorship endorses the account.

- [Nature, 21 April 1898, Societies and Academies](https://www.nature.com/articles/057599a0)

The Antarctic discussion reported in March 1898 has Sir John Evans presiding
in Lord Lister's absence, with the chair directing discussion towards
scientific matters. Chapter III uses an unnamed Vice-President, a brief
introduction, and questions deferred until the account ends, with one brief
clarification about shade nevertheless breaking into the address. It invents no
verbatim historical procedure or named participant.

- [Nature, 3 March 1898, The Scientific Advantages of an Antarctic Expedition](https://www.nature.com/articles/057413a0.pdf)

The Medical Man's fortnight is a minimum recovery interval. The meeting falls
on the Thursday after it has elapsed. The full machine remains at Richmond;
notebook, samples, specimen, drawing and prints are the immediate exhibits.

## Camera and specimens

The 1896 Eastman catalogue describes a Pocket Kodak with twelve exposures per
roll, numbered backing paper, a winding key and daylight loading. It also
offers development and printing after exposure. A single early roll can
therefore hold the three baseline views and three repeated Station II views.
No field darkroom is needed to advance the film; the Traveller cannot inspect
the resulting photographs in the field. The manuscript keeps the camera
unnamed and describes the view through its finder, not a developed image.

- [1896 Eastman catalogue, printed pp. 13–15](https://www.pacificrimcamera.com/rl/00116/00116.pdf)
- [Science Museum: Pocket Kodak camera, introduced 1895](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co15226/pocket-kodak-camera)

Later drafting must get the early roll out of the camera and secure it before
the final photographic material is lost. That handling is not narrated in
Chapter III. Station II samples are collected, but the agreed surviving
comparison samples are baseline and Station III; this chapter does not add
Station II bottles or air to the surviving exhibits.

## Station II constraints

- About thirteen months after baseline; roughly one day and night occupied.
- Far bank invisible before nine; shaded thermometer 104 at two; stream two
  feet three inches below its notch; lake margin sixty paces beyond its mark.
- The lack of a flood-mark is interpreted by the Traveller as proof against
  seasonality. It does not establish continuous low water during his absence.
  The later examination must retain that distinction.
- Fish remain in the deeper reach; vegetation persists but looks duller.
  Faunal collapse and major vegetation failure belong to later chapters.
- His imagined upstream diversion gives the water recession and exposed dusty
  ground a local explanation. He has not traced a river–lake connection; their
  common decline seems to confirm his assumption. Looking from the cairn finds
  no new course. Neither the diversion nor its location is an established fact.
  Chapter IV can test its adequacy against changes beyond the water margins.
- The scarred predator drinks on the opposite bank and watches him. Its
  crossing to his bank belongs to Station III.
- The plumb remains hanging in the refuge through the next advance. No roof
  grains fall here; that observation is already assigned to Station III, where
  the notebook explicitly leaves both chalk line and plumb unaltered.
- The next advance is ten months, reaching about twenty-three months after
  baseline. The choice is made here; arrival belongs to Chapter IV.
  He now allows that fuller seasonal water could re-enter an abandoned bed,
  rather than assuming a different season must undo a permanent diversion.
