# Epilogue Period and Continuity Brief

Astra's first draft, closed after Fable's Wells-1895 calibration and four
local repairs (Correspondence message 524, thread 55). This brief records the
choices made in the coda and the limits that remain in force. The complete
story is ready for Barry's read.

## Scene and frame

The conversation takes place on the Saturday after the Thursday meeting, over
cigars and smoking bishop at Richmond. This fixes an interval within the
existing timeline without introducing a calendar date. The narrator observes
the exchange and joins it briefly; Filby remains an interlocutor. Mrs.
Watchett's footsteps are heard, but she does not join the conversation. The
existing `richmond-study` location includes the laboratory.

The acquaintance who has moved to Italy is new, minor domestic information.
His move makes the Traveller's three-year absence felt without supplying a
new episode or a historical claim. The drink and the private setting come
from the approved Act V design. No recipe, new apparatus specification, or
additional claim about Victorian science is introduced.

## What the examination has changed

The Geologist's objection survives in full: more observations within one
valley would not establish what happened elsewhere on Earth. The Traveller
abandons an attempted rejoinder himself, then admits that the rest of the
world might have continued much as usual. His continued disbelief in that
possibility remains his judgement, not the narrator's finding.

The Geologist is not identified as Filby's communicating Fellow; that Fellow
remains the Anatomist of III and VI. The dial's large figure appears only in
the Traveller's recollection of the Geologist's reaction. Neither the date
nor the extinction mechanism acquires fresh authority.

The photographic regret concerns the baseline view towards the plain in II,
where the Traveller already doubts his exposures. He wishes he had walked
nearer rather than relying upon a distant view. This is a new recollection
of his impatience, not a new photograph or a lost chemical result. Filby
presses him until he admits that better local photographs would still leave
the Geologist's central objection intact.

## Return, obstruction and condition

- I promises a wait until half past twelve, then until dinner; II returns
  him at four and has him carried upstairs. The dinner exchange preserves
  both the clock and the difference between arriving and arriving well.
- III establishes the stiff right finger and thumb. They remain stiff here;
  the coda does not certify recovery. The bent rail remains unstraightened.
- I supplies the dust in the laboratory after the three-year absence and the
  conjecture that the early experiments caused the obstruction. The coda adds
  his private fear of another delayed return and another changed household.
  It supplies no new width, location, measurement, trial, or mechanism.
- Fear of returning is explicitly distinguished from the reason he stayed:
  he still says he stayed because he thought he understood the developing
  crisis. The coda does not replace IV–V's account of that decision.
- The narrator's momentary unease about the laboratory door is his own
  association, not evidence of the anomaly's present extent. The explicit
  explanation of that association was cut at Fable's suggestion.
- The Richmond deputation remains pending. The Traveller expects the
  Astronomer to come and intends to tell him about the obstruction; no visit,
  undertaking to demonstrate the machine, or result is narrated.
- The fish, samples, photographs, notebook and receipt retain VI's custody.
  None is newly displayed or transferred in the coda.

## The single sign of preparation

The selected sign is a pencilled paper scale curved against one existing
dial, with some divisions erased and redrawn closer together. The dial is
not identified. No setting, numerical value, destination, or new component
is supplied, and the paper does not certify improved precision or a working
repair. There are no accompanying packed supplies, new maps, or completed
calculations to turn the hint into an announced expedition.

Filby's final act is to lower the lamp so the Traveller can see the scale.
Whether that is assistance, curiosity, or reluctant complicity is left to
the reader. He makes no promise to approve another journey. The Traveller
first holds the lamp up for the rail's examination; the earlier direction
"well away from the frame" was removed because it suggested concealment.

## Voice and review

Primary comparison: the domestic dialogue of *The Time Machine* I and the
return to the listeners and machine in XII. The `reflection-and-closure`
packet supplies a bounded check on ending the available record while leaving
a supported uncertainty. The original epilogue's destinations, flowers and
concluding cadence are not carried over.

Fable passed the domestic voices, the Geologist's surviving objection, the
Traveller's private fear and the final gesture. He resolved the syntax
caution by comparing the coda with Wells's dinner conversation and the XII
tail; the whole-account upper sentence tail was the wrong comparator here.
Nothing was lengthened to meet it, and no dashes were added to meet a count.

Closeout removes the narrator's interpretation and pause before Filby's
photograph question, the abstract responsibility exchange (including the
Traveller's now-dependent reply), and the door paragraph's self-explanation.
The lamp is simply held up for the first examination. The glass against the
Traveller's cheek remains deliberate: he wants its warmth, as he wants more
coal despite Filby's complaint. Fable asked for no further retest cycle.
