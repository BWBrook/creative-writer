# 1. Executive findings

1. **Established — the “week” should be personal, not global.** Short traverses can sample a longer local deterioration while the Traveller experiences about seven days. The principal marine extinction interval is resolved at roughly 60,000 years, and terrestrial crises were diachronous. The Geologist should accept the ordered local collapse but resist a literal seven-day planetary event. ([Burgess, Bowring & Shen 2014](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1317692111); [Dal Corso et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43017-021-00259-4); [Wu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adi7284))

2. **Supported — ancient Richmond is best placed on the exposed London–Brabant structural high.** BGS syntheses describe the London region as probably a rocky Permian desert and the massif as the southern upland margin of the Southern Permian Basin. Permian strata are absent on its crest. ([BGS London](https://webapps.bgs.ac.uk/memoirs/docs/B01360.html); [BGS Southern North Sea](https://webapps.bgs.ac.uk/memoirs/docs/B01848.html))

3. **Supported — use a low-latitude seasonal desert, approximately 15–25°N.** “About 20°N” is a useful modern shorthand, but plate assignment, palaeomagnetic error and erased local strata forbid single-degree or kilometre-scale precision. ([van Hinsbergen et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0346817); [Müller et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018GC007584))

4. **Supported — the mixed landscape is defensible after one adjustment.** Prefer rocky ridge or scarp, alluvial plain, substantial seasonal river and terminal saline lake/playa. A confidently nearby open Zechstein coast at Richmond is weaker.

5. **Supported — use a shallow fissure-cave or rock shelter, not mature karst.** A joint, fault, undercut resistant bed or talus-roofed recess can house the machine without inventing a deep limestone system.

6. **Supported — make flood-assisted undercutting and roof fall the climax mechanism.** Vegetation loss and erosion prepare the catchment; exceptional rain causes sheet flood, channel avulsion, debris movement and shelter failure. A small local tremor is the only reserve option.

7. **Established/Supported — Siberian Traps magmatism is the leading ultimate cause, but unknowable as “Siberia” from Richmond.** The Traveller may infer vast distant volcanic action; he cannot identify its continent, smell its source or perceive isotope and metal proxies. ([Burgess & Bowring 2015](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.1500470); [Burgess, Muirhead & Bowring 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-00083-9))

8. **Established — the restrained mechanism backbone is carbon-cycle disruption, greenhouse warming and marine oxygen loss.** At Richmond, use heat, water stress, vegetation failure, erosion and hydrological extremes. Ozone damage, metals, universal wildfire and atmospheric hydrogen sulphide should remain contested background possibilities. ([Penn et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat1327); [Dal Corso et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43017-021-00259-4))

9. **Supported — sample roughly two to five real years, then remain continuously present for the final 48–72 hours.** This is long enough for ecological direction and short enough for the same predator. Millennial leaps quietly destroy individual continuity.

10. **Supported — the complete specimen should be a small palaeoniscid-grade fish.** *Palaeoniscus*, *Acrolepis* and “ganoid fish” were period-available; a complete fish fits a spirit jar and permits serious anatomical examination. British Marl Slate fossils are an older regional analogue, not a Richmond inventory. ([JNCC fishes](https://data.jncc.gov.uk/data/f722dcd3-4cf1-46d8-9624-56abff537c8d/gcr-v16-fossil-fishes-of-great-britain-c10.pdf))

11. **Speculative — use an unnamed medium-large theriodont-grade predator.** “Theriodont”, *Gorgonops* and *Lycosaurus* were available by the 1890s, but no terminal-Permian Richmond occurrence exists. Call it theriodont-like or a carnivorous anomodont; never import *Inostrancevia*. ([Owen 1876](https://books.google.com/books?id=FPJeaTdtACMC); [Lydekker 1890](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/item/125712))

12. **Established — Wells’s own scientific idiom supports observation before naming.** His biology text emphasises precise observation, annotated drawing, comparison, deduction and verification, and uses “conditions of life”, “Permo-triassic”, “mammal-like reptiles”, “living equilibrium” and “extinction”. (Wells, *Text-Book of Biology*, attached project text.)

13. **Supported — strong spirit is the conservative preservative; formalin is authentic but conspicuously new.** Formaldehyde fixation entered scientific practice in the 1890s and would invite questions about hardening and contamination. ([Musiał et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28275269/))

14. **Supported — recommend a specially extended ordinary Royal Society meeting at Burlington House.** A Fellow communicates the paper; the chair regulates questions; specimens sit on the table. A conversazione suits exhibits but not sustained examination, and the Friday Evening Discourse belongs to the Royal Institution. ([Royal Society *Record*, 1897](https://archive.org/details/recordofroyalsoc00royauoft); [Conversazione 1898](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7.178.738))

15. **Established — leave the machine’s anchoring and rematerialisation physics unexplained.** Canon supports both temporal directions, large and small intervals, exhaustion, fixed locality, removable levers and lethal stopping inside matter; it does not solve attachment to Earth, cargo protection or extreme-age calibration. (Wells, *The Time Machine*, attached project text.)

# 2. Method and limitations

The approved story design is treated as fixed: five acts, concentric testimony, terrestrial anchoring, costly local traverses, a deliberately collected specimen, an ordinary predator under extraordinary pressure, a geologically motivated refuge failure, cumulative evidence and a divided Royal Society verdict. Modern science is therefore a guardrail, not a replacement architecture. (*The Last Week of the Permian Story Design*, attached project text.)

The modern layer draws on peer-reviewed geochronology, volcanology, palaeoclimate and extinction research; BGS regional geology; JNCC faunal syntheses; and current plate/palaeolatitude infrastructure. The historical layer prioritises pre-1900 geology, palaeontology, chemistry, institutional records and Wells’s own biology text. Later histories are used chiefly to verify dates and changing meanings.

The largest limitation is absence of evidence at Richmond: no Permian deposits are preserved beneath the London–Thames region. Broad structure and climate can be reconstructed, but not a local formation, river, shoreline, cave or fauna. Organisms must be guild-based, with named north-west European forms treated as analogues. ([BGS London](https://webapps.bgs.ac.uk/memoirs/docs/B01360.html))

Palaeolatitude tools constrain latitude, not longitude, topography or shore distance. The 15–25°N envelope is deliberately rounded; no exact Richmond API value is claimed. The formal Permian–Triassic boundary is also not identical with extinction onset or completion: it is a biostratigraphic datum at Meishan. ([van Hinsbergen et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0346817); [ICS GSSP](https://stratigraphy.org/gssps/files/induan.pdf))

Terms were admitted only when attested by 1899 and used with their period meaning. Behavioural claims are labelled inference: fossils can establish occurrence and anatomy, not indifference, hunger or refuge-seeking. Where scans or taxonomic histories did not support exact wording, this report paraphrases rather than fabricating quotation or page number.

# 3. Recommended local reconstruction

## Preferred setting

**Supported.** Place the machine on the exposed London–Brabant high at approximately 15–25°N. A low rocky ridge or faulted scarp overlooks an alluvial plain with a substantial ephemeral river, abandoned pools and a terminal saline lake or playa. Patchy conifer and seed-fern scrub occupies better-watered ground; denser vegetation follows channels. Salt crusts, red sediment and bare Palaeozoic rock supply contrasting substrates. BGS directly supports a rocky desert high; the river–playa mosaic is a basin-margin analogue introduced to satisfy the approved aquatic requirement without inventing a sea. ([BGS London](https://webapps.bgs.ac.uk/memoirs/docs/B01360.html); [BGS Southern North Sea](https://webapps.bgs.ac.uk/memoirs/docs/B01848.html))

The refuge is a shallow weathered cleft: a joint or minor fault, an undercut resistant bed, an abandoned channel notch or a talus-roofed recess. The Traveller may call it a cave; the geological description should be “fissure-cave” or “rock shelter”.

## One alternative

**Contested.** Place the tract on the northern flank rather than the crest of the massif, permitting a more persistent saline lake. This improves aquatic continuity but increases model dependence and should still not become a precisely mapped Zechstein coast.

No exact altitude, river direction, distance to water, named formation or cave lithology is defensible. The Traveller can measure relative height and bearings while admitting that absolute geography is unknown.

# 4. Modern scientific reconstruction

## 4.1 Place and climate

At the end of the Permian, southern Britain occupied the northern low latitudes within Pangaea. The London–Brabant Massif was an exposed structural high forming the southern margin of the Southern Permian Basin; aeolian, fluvial and lacustrine deposits accumulated northward, followed by repeated Zechstein marine transgressions. ([Müller et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018GC007584); [BGS Southern North Sea](https://webapps.bgs.ac.uk/memoirs/docs/B01848.html))

The regional analogue is a hot, strongly seasonal continental desert rather than uninterrupted dunes. Rotliegend records include rivers, desert lakes, sabkhas, halite and sheet floods. Relief should be modest — tens to perhaps a few hundred metres across the visible landscape — because “structural high” need not mean mountain.

## 4.2 Timing

The formal boundary lies at the first appearance of *Hindeodus parvus* in Bed 27c at Meishan. It is a correlation datum, not the instant all Permian organisms vanished. The principal Meishan marine extinction interval spans approximately 251.941 to 251.880 Ma, about 60 kyr at maximum likelihood, while terrestrial records differ in timing and pulse structure. ([ICS GSSP](https://stratigraphy.org/gssps/files/induan.pdf); [Burgess, Bowring & Shen 2014](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1317692111); [Wu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adi7284))

## 4.3 Mechanisms

Siberian Traps magmatism is the leading ultimate driver. High-precision dates place major magmatism before, during and after extinction, and align widespread sill intrusion into volatile-rich Tunguska Basin sediments with the main crisis. Massive volcanic carbon release caused greenhouse warming and carbon-cycle disruption. In the ocean, warming reduced oxygen availability while increasing animal demand, producing widespread deoxygenation and anoxia. ([Burgess & Bowring 2015](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.1500470); [Burgess, Muirhead & Bowring 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-00083-9); [Cui et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2014701118); [Penn et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat1327))

Acidification is supported but not directly observable from an inland station. On land, heat, altered seasonality, vegetation stress, erosion, local wildfire and extreme runoff form a defensible cascade, but their timing varied geographically. Ozone damage, UV stress, toxic metals and atmospheric hydrogen sulphide remain contested amplifiers, not a required disaster list. ([Dal Corso et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43017-021-00259-4); [Vajda et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsl.2019.115875))

## 4.4 Observable local sequence

| Stage | Real interval | Observable development |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Baseline | arrival | Seasonal river/playa, patchy scrub, ordinary fauna; predator passes without interest; specimen collected |
| 2. Recurrent irregularity | weeks–months | Higher maxima and warmer nights, haze, altered winds/pressure, earlier water retreat |
| 3. Aquatic stress | months | Warm turbid pools, salt concentration, fish/crustacean crowding, black mud and local putrefactive or sulphurous odour |
| 4. Vegetation failure | months–year | Wilting, shedding, exposed roots, dust and reduced insect activity |
| 5. Faunal displacement | months | Herbivores move along water, carcasses and scavenging increase, familiar forms diminish |
| 6. Optional local fire | days | One drought-conditioned burn supplies soot and erosion preconditioning; omit if crowded |
| 7. Hydrological reversal | weeks–months | Falling pressure, violent rain, sheet flood, channel avulsion and debris-laden water |
| 8. Continuous climax | final 48–72 h | Hungry/displaced predator seeks water, shade or carrion near fissure while flood undercuts entrance and roof fails |

This sequence should cover roughly two to five real years. Repeated variables — temperature, pressure, water level/quality, plant condition, animal counts and fissure geometry — are stronger than a new mechanism at every stop.

## 4.5 Limits of inference

The Traveller can establish an ordered local collapse and argue that prolonged heat and haze imply forcing larger than ordinary weather. Absence of a local vent makes distant volcanism plausible. He cannot locate Siberia, quantify global carbon, prove simultaneous marine extinction, identify every dust source or show that one mechanism killed all taxa. His strongest defensible claim is that a powerful external forcing altered climate and water, propagating through vegetation, prey, predators and slope stability.

# 5. Late-1890s knowledge and language

## 5.1 Period knowledge

Murchison proposed the “Permian System” in 1841. By the 1890s British geologists placed it between the Carboniferous and Trias, while recognising difficult correlations and striking faunal change. Ramsay described red beds, magnesian limestone, sparse fossils and inland salt-lake conditions analogous to the Caspian. ([Benton et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1144/jgs2021-037); [Ramsay 1878](https://www.geology.19thcenturyscience.org/books/1878-Ramsay-Geology/htm/doc.html))

Wells’s biology text already refers to “Permo-triassic strata”, “reptile-like mammals”, “mammal-like reptiles”, adaptation, extinction and a “living equilibrium”. A credible initiating lecture is therefore “the apparent cessation of the Permian fauna and the physical conditions attending the passage to the Trias”, not a modern quantified “mass extinction”.

Victorian geology could combine ordinary causes over deep time with exceptional volcanism, inundation, subsidence and climatic alteration. “Catastrophe” need not mean suspension of natural law. Tyndall and Arrhenius made heat absorption by gases and warming by atmospheric carbonic acid available to an unusually current investigator, but not field measurement of global CO₂. ([Tyndall 1861](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstl.1861.0001); [Arrhenius 1896](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786449608620846))

Continental drift, Pangaea, radiometric dating and the Siberian Traps extinction model were unavailable. A tropical Richmond could be attributed to former land-and-sea distribution, elevation/subsidence or even movement of the pole; the modern plate explanation remains backstage.

Owen and Lydekker made “Theriodontia”, “Anomodontia”, *Gorgonops*, *Lycosaurus*, dicynodonts and labyrinthodonts available. *Protorosaurus* was known from British/German Upper Permian material. The animal now called *Adelosaurus huxleyi* remained *Proterosaurus huxleyi* until 1914. ([Owen 1876](https://books.google.com/books?id=FPJeaTdtACMC); [Lydekker 1890](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/item/125712); [JNCC reptiles](https://data.jncc.gov.uk/data/574eac76-683c-47c6-a1de-b22439031f4c/gcr-v10-fossil-reptiles-of-great-britain-c3.pdf))

## 5.3 Wellsian translation

Wells’s scientific rhythm is “thing before name”: observe external form, define terms, compare with familiar types, infer function, and state doubt explicitly. The notebook should therefore measure the pool before calling it poisoned, describe the fish before naming it, and revise a simple first explanation as evidence accumulates. His textbook’s “probably”, “would appear”, “some ground for thinking” and “considerable doubt” are authentic resources, not weakness.

### Recommendation: a palaeoniscid-grade fish

Take an undamaged 12–20 cm fish during the calm phase, not a last survivor. The Traveller records rhomboidal scales, heterocercal tail, fins, gill arches and teeth; the Comparative Anatomist may compare it with *Palaeoniscus* and *Acrolepis* while declining species identification. The British synthesis supports morphology and period familiarity, not identity with a Marl Slate marine species. ([JNCC British Permian fishes](https://data.jncc.gov.uk/data/f722dcd3-4cf1-46d8-9624-56abff537c8d/gcr-v16-fossil-fishes-of-great-britain-c10.pdf))

### Recommendation: unnamed theriodont-grade predator

Use no modern species name. A lean, long-skulled quadruped with differentiated teeth and one or two enlarged canine-like teeth permits comparison with Owen’s theriodonts and *Gorgonops*. External form, tracks and feeding remains support “carnivorous”; they do not warrant a genus.

At baseline it notices the unfamiliar biped and moves on because he is neither customary prey nor rival. As water and prey fail, its range expands; food, specimen odour, shade or water draws it towards the fissure. Heat, thirst, injury and hunger lower its risk threshold without producing malice or strategic obsession. This is the dossier’s largest palaeontological liberty, but period-authentic uncertainty is preferable to falsely placing a famous Russian or South African species in Surrey.

# 8. Refuge and climax options

## Primary: fissure-cave in an undercut scarp, failed by flood and roof fall

**Supported.** The machine is dragged into a weathered cleft where a resistant bed or block forms a shallow roof above a weaker, jointed or rubbly base. The entrance looks stable during the baseline phase and remains above ordinary channel floods. The Traveller establishes a measured station: a painted or scratched water-level staff outside; thermometer and barometer inside; fixed nails or chalk marks across cracks; sample shelves in a dry recess; machine facing outward with its lever side accessible.

Progressive loss of vegetation increases runoff and sediment mobility. During the final low-pressure episode, intense rain falls on the denuded catchment. A distributary or wadi avulses toward the scarp. Water and debris scour the entrance, saturate fine material beneath a roof block and deposit a wedge around the machine’s frame. The first slab fall narrows access but does not conveniently destroy the predator. The displaced animal approaches because the fissure offers shade, elevated dry ground, prey odour or temporary escape from the flood. Its pressure forces the Traveller to work before the shelter has finished failing.

The climax can then use one causal chain rather than independent hazards: environmental deterioration strips the land; the stripped land turns rain into destructive runoff; runoff destabilises the shelter; the same collapse displaces animals and channels the predator toward the refuge. The Traveller clears the control side and enough frame to operate, abandons bulky evidence and accepts the canonical rematerialisation risk because waiting is worse.

The physical description should avoid a cinematic chasm opening to magma or a perfectly timed total cave-in. More credible precursors are trickling grains, a new crack, a shifted plumb line, dull impacts within the roof, water emerging from a joint, a slab rotating rather than exploding and a mud/debris wedge that makes manual access difficult.

## Fallback: tremor-assisted joint failure

**Speculative.** Retain the same fissure and flood, but allow a modest local tremor to loosen an already undercut block. The tremor need not be tied directly to Siberian eruptions; intraplate stress, local fault adjustment or simple uncertainty is enough. The Traveller may interpret it as evidence of widespread subterranean disturbance, while the Geologist later calls that inference unwarranted. Do not use sustained earthquakes, local volcanism or a seismic wave explicitly crossing half Pangaea from Siberia.

# 9. Victorian expedition kit and exhibits

## 9.1 Compact field kit

The open saddle and delicate nickel, ivory and crystal structure suggest two stout panniers or portmanteaux, not a laboratory cart. Canon itself supplies the lesson: on the first journey the Traveller regretted lacking arms, medicine, tobacco, sufficient matches and a Kodak. (Wells, *The Time Machine*, attached project text, ch. ## 9.2 Containers and preservation

| Material | Method | Evidentiary use and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Air | Flame-sealed glass tubes or double-stopcock gas tubes, with a Richmond control | Later qualitative comparison only; leakage, adsorption, pressure and sealing contaminate composition |
| Water | Numbered glass-stopper bottles; untreated and reagent-fixed aliquots | Salinity residue, acidity, ions and microscopic contents; storage changes gases, microbes and colour |
| Mineral, soot and salt | Glazed-paper packets inside tins, with waxed labels | Microscopy, solubility and qualitative chemistry; source seldom diagnostic |
| Plants | Small press with blotters; duplicate fragments in spirit | Form and venation; one leaf cannot identify the injury mechanism |
| Arthropods | Pin and dry in cork box with camphor; minute forms in spirit | Anatomy and examples of abundance; fragile in transit |
| Complete fish | Wide-mouthed thick jar in strong spirit, stopper secured and packed in felt | Whole anatomy and associated water; spirit extracts colour and alters tissues |
| Tissue fragments | Spirit; paraffin embedding and Canada-balsam mounting after return | Microscopy belongs chiefly in the Richmond laboratory |

Strong spirit is the conservative choice. Wells’s practical syllabus uses methylated or strong spirit, turpentine, paraffin wax, oil of cloves and Canada balsam. Formalin is possible after its 1890s introduction, but it is conspicuously novel, hardens tissue and complicates chemical interpretation; use it only if cutting-edge technique is narratively useful. Arsenical soap belongs to skins, drying to plants, and pinning to insects. ([Musiał et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28275269/); Wells, *Text-Book of Biology*, attached project text.)

## 9.3 Sample discipline and survival

At each stop take the same compact series: air A, water W, sediment S, plant P, meteorological readings, a fixed-point photograph where possible, and brief visible-animal counts. Labels carry stop number and relative dial increment, not a false calendar date; Richmond blanks travel with the kit.

The surviving exhibits should be the notebook and instrument register, complete fish, a few early/middle/late air and water samples, several deposit packets, one pressed-plant pair and perhaps photographs. Credibly lost are duplicates, plant press, bulk rocks, most reagents, spare provisions, camera, excavation tools and the late sample crate. The loss must remove precisely the evidence that might have separated competing causal explanations.

# 10. Royal Society examination

## 10.1 Setting recommendation

**Supported.** Use a specially extended ordinary scientific meeting at the Royal Society’s Burlington House apartments, during the November–June session, on a Thursday afternoon. The President or a Vice-President chairs; an FRS formally communicates the paper if the Traveller is not a Fellow; Fellows and a limited number of introduced visitors attend. ([Royal Society, *Record*, 1897](https://archive.org/details/recordofroyalsoc00royauoft); [contemporary meeting notice](https://www.nature.com/articles/057322a0.pdf))

This is not a modern conference: no slide deck, microphone, name badges or formal queue. The Traveller speaks from notes; objects occupy the table; the chair recognises questioners. A conversazione is suitable for displaying specimens but not the sustained examination, while a Friday Evening Discourse belongs to the Royal Institution. ([Royal Society Conversazione 1898](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7.178.738))

## 10.2 Presentation of evidence

Leave the full machine at Richmond. Present the small model or damaged component, removable levers and drawings; witnesses to the model, departures and returns; original notebooks and instrument register; numbered samples; the sealed fish; and photographs or drawings. Fellows may inspect the full machine later by deputation. Nothing is juridical, and no single object proves time travel: credibility is cumulative.

## 10.3 Questions by occupational role

### Geologist

1. “By what independent character do you call the ground later Permian rather than older New Red or passage-beds of the Trias: strata, characteristic fossils, or chiefly the machine?”
2. “How do you distinguish extinction from migration, season or destruction of one drainage basin?”
3. “Which powder is demonstrably volcanic rather than dust, fire residue or comminuted local rock?”
4. “Given uncertain extreme calibration, which intervals are measured, and which observations were continuous?”

These questions separate dial age from stratigraphy, local disappearance from general extinction, and volcanic convergence from proof.

### Astronomer

1. “The Earth rotates, orbits, and moves with the Sun. Why are you not left where the Earth has ceased to be?” The Traveller reports terrestrial anchoring and may invoke gravitation or attachment to matter, but cannot solve it.
2. “How did solar altitude establish latitude after correction for season, precession, refraction and possible polar displacement?”
3. “What joins one revolution of the remotest dial to a terrestrial day when the heavens become bands and arcs?”
4. “Why must a reddened sun at one station indicate a planetary atmosphere rather than local smoke or dust?”

### Comparative Anatomist

1. “Do scales, tail and fin supports agree with *Palaeoniscus* or *Acrolepis*, and which characters has spirit obscured?”
2. “What colour, softness or injury preceded preservation?”
3. “Did you see the predator’s cranial openings, palate or jaw articulation, or only canine-like teeth and habit of body?”
4. “How does repeated sight establish famine-induced disposition rather than different individuals or ordinary variation?”

The proper result is “ganoid fish closely allied to known forms” and “theriodont-like predator”, not miraculous species diagnoses.

### Chemist or natural philosopher

1. “Were the air tubes flame-sealed or stoppered, and is there a Richmond blank enclosed by the same operation?”
2. “What quantity do limewater, lead paper and litmus establish, and could spirit, cork, decay or lamp fumes produce the reaction?”
3. “Was water tested before storage, since a closed bottle is itself a small chemical world?”
4. “What connects carbonic acid, sulphurous matter or sulphuretted hydrogen with one remote volcanic source rather than local strata, fire or decay?”

## 10.4 Expected outcome

The meeting may provisionally accept a remote journey and genuinely anomalous material while leaving the date broad and the global volcanic interpretation disputed. The fish, sample sequence and notebooks change the burden of explanation without compelling unanimity; that mixed verdict fits both Victorian scientific practice and the approved ending.

# 11. Canonical machine constraints

Only the attached *The Time Machine* is used here.

| Canonical evidence | Story constraint |
|---|---|
| The model and full machine have separate starting and stopping/reversing levers; the Traveller can remove the small control levers and pocket them (chs I and IV–V) | The Permian machine can be disabled against accidental activation; controls can be carried into the lecture. Do not invent a dashboard of modern controls. |
| The machine travels into both future and past in principle, although the model’s direction is initially uncertain (ch. I) | A deep journey into the past does not contradict canon. Exact target accuracy is not established. |
| During travel the machine becomes indistinct or effectively invisible because its presentation is diluted below perception (chs I and III) | Observers and animals need not see it normally while it is moving rapidly through time. This does not make the stopped machine invisible. |
| The Traveller remains “on the hillside upon which this house now stands” while buildings, vegetation and landscape change (ch. III) | Strong support for fixed local position in the terrestrial frame; the story’s crustal anchoring is an extrapolation, not an explained mechanism. |
| High-speed travel produces falling, swaying, visual pain, mental confusion and exhaustion (chs III and XI) | Repeated short traverses should be physically costly, especially after deep travel and poor sleep. |
| Stopping where matter occupies the same space risks molecular interpenetration, chemical reaction and explosion; an abrupt stop overturns the machine and throws him (ch. III) | Every cave stop requires occupiable clearance. The Traveller cannot safely stop inside a roof block, flood deposit, animal or newly shifted wall. |
| Dials record days and very large multiples; the Traveller makes a one-month jump, a hundred-year jump and later thousand-year strides (ch. XI) | Short comparative traverses and enormous deep-time intervals are canonically possible. Dial existence does not guarantee perfect extreme-age calibration. |
| The machine can be pushed or carried in ordinary space when stopped, and Morlocks move it with the levers removed (chs V and X) | Dragging it into a nearby shelter is permitted, though labour should be considerable. |
| Morlocks oil, clean and perhaps partly dismantle it without destroying its function (ch. X) | The structure is mechanically accessible and not protected by an impenetrable field; dust, mud and roof debris can obstruct it. |
| The Traveller brings two flowers back, and clothing/dirt travel with him (Epilogue and ch. II) | Small carried objects and samples can return. Canon gives no quantitative cargo limit. |
| He regrets lacking a Kodak, medicines, weapons and sufficient matches (ch. VI) | A later expedition kit is source-motivated, not an imported steampunk convention. |

The following questions should remain deliberately vague:

- why the machine remains with Earth despite rotation, orbit and solar motion;
- whether anchoring is gravitational, material, inertial or something else;
- how the machine selects the same crustal tract through continental deformation;
- how much empty volume is needed to stop safely;
- whether adjacent loose objects are protected equally or only when securely carried;
- how the dials remain calibrated over approximately 252 million years;
- what happens if the shelter is only partly occupiable at the chosen stop;
- whether changing ground level produces vertical adjustment or merely leaves the machine at risk.

The Astronomer may formulate the first problem more clearly than the Traveller. The Traveller’s strongest answer is empirical: the machine behaves as though attached to terrestrial matter or gravitation, but his invention works before its physical theory is complete.

# 13. Intentional-divergence register

These are provisional opportunities, not creative decisions already made.

1. **Ramsay-style inland salt-lake causation — provisional.** The Traveller may interpret the setting through Caspian-like continental basins and poisonous concentrated waters. Modern science would treat that local hydrology as an expression of a global volcanic–carbon crisis rather than the ultimate cause. This divergence is period-authentic and visually useful.

2. **Volcanic exhalation as a comparatively direct poison — provisional.** Victorian chemistry may lead him to place greater causal weight on sulphurous gases or carbonic acid at ground level than modern evidence supports. The Chemist should separate observed reaction from concentration and source.

3. **A universal termination inferred from one complete local sequence — provisional.** The Traveller’s confidence and the title can encourage overreach; the Geologist’s strongest contribution is to retain the local fact while rejecting global proof.

4. **Theriodont identification from external resemblance — provisional.** The Traveller can call the predator theriodont with more confidence than modern standards permit. The Comparative Anatomist reduces this to a grade-level comparison.

5. **Mammalian ancestry expressed as a linear approach — provisional.** Wells’s textbook naturally speaks of “reptile-like mammals” and transitional forms. Modern phylogeny would replace a ladder with branching relationships. The manuscript may retain the period framing without making the animal literally half mammal.

6. **The doubtful *Lepidotosaurus* as a live point of debate — provisional.** A Society specialist might compare an observation with the supposed British labyrinthodont *Lepidotosaurus*. Modern review suggests the specimen may instead be a fish. This is a productive period-authentic uncertainty, but it should not become the named predator.

7. **Carbonic-acid warming treated as rapidly demonstrable — provisional.** Arrhenius makes the general idea available, but the Traveller cannot measure atmospheric abundance. He may be right in mechanism and overconfident in proof.

8. **Machine age treated as more exact than geology permits — provisional.** He may report a precise dial total while admitting accumulated error. The Geologist’s refusal to treat it as an independent chronometer preserves the historical gap.

9. **Pole or geography speculation instead of continental drift — provisional.** Faced with a tropical Richmond, the Traveller or Astronomer may invoke altered pole, crustal movement, elevation or a changed arrangement of seas. Modern science recognises plate motion; the period manuscript need not.

10. **Catastrophic rate within uniform natural law — recommended.** The Traveller may describe a geological “catastrophe” while insisting that it arose through heat, gas, water, erosion and animal need. This is not a mistake but a historically fruitful distinction from caricatured catastrophism.

# 14. Open decisions

1. **Latitude wording:** retain the dossier’s broad “15–25°N, approximately 20°N under representative models”, or keep all dialogue to “near the northern tropic”.

2. **Water body:** choose the preferred terminal saline lake/large seasonal channel, or adopt the more model-dependent northern-flank substantial lake alternative.

3. **Predator taxonomic confidence:** use only “theriodont-like” in the notebook and allow “theriodont” at the lecture, or let the Traveller begin overconfidently with *Gorgonops* and retreat under examination.

4. **Preservative:** use conservative strong spirit throughout, or make the Traveller unusually current by using brief formalin fixation before spirit storage.

5. **Photography:** carry a Pocket Kodak and lose the apparatus while retaining a few exposures, or omit it to keep evidence more tactile and notebook-centred.

6. **Real duration:** set the sampled deterioration at approximately two, three or five years. Two to five years supports the same predator; a longer interval should abandon individual continuity.

7. **Fire:** include one restrained local burn/soot stage, or omit it so that heat, water failure and flood remain the sole physical line.

8. **Reserve tremor:** allow one small tremor to assist an already failing roof, or keep the climax entirely hydrological and gravitational.

9. **Meeting form:** call the event a specially extended ordinary meeting, or leave its exceptional administrative status implicit while preserving Burlington House procedure.

10. **Surviving evidence:** decide whether photographs survive; the minimum scientifically effective set is notebook, instrument register, fish, three air tubes, two water bottles, four deposit packets and one pressed plant pair.

## Closing research judgement

The smallest coherent choice is a low-latitude rocky high overlooking an ephemeral river and terminal saline water body, sampled across a few years of worsening heat, water failure, vegetation loss and animal displacement. One exceptional storm turns accumulated damage into flood, debris movement and roof failure. A complete small ganoid fish supplies tangible evidence; an unnamed theriodont-grade predator supplies ordinary animal danger. The Traveller may be substantially right about distant volcanic action and widespread organic crisis while unable to date it independently, locate its source, separate every mechanism or prove planetary extinction from ancient Richmond alone.
