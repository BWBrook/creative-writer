# The Scarred Hunter

## Scene purpose

Tie the changing animal behaviour to the same environmental deterioration and
raise personal danger without converting the predator into a villain.

## POV

The Traveller observes the predator's condition, distance, attention, and
relation to displaced prey through field comparison rather than mind-reading.

## Pressure

The animal is leaner and more willing to remain near the Traveller. He must
decide whether the shoulder scar proves identity and whether its behaviour
signals local scarcity or a larger collapse.

## Turn

He provisionally treats it as the same animal and adds faunal desperation to
his widening hypothesis of powerful distant volcanic action.

## Exit state

The predator has become a foreseeable risk, and the causal claim has become
strong enough to deserve one final temporal test.

## Continuity hooks

- Identity remains challengeable in Chapter VI.
- The animal does not attack in this scene.
- Distant volcanism is an inference; no source, ash fall, or modern tectonic
  map confirms it.
