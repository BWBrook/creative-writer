## Observations

### Observation 1

#### Passage
- `the-invisible-man:iii:92:8c3779ecfb78`

#### Observation
The account distributes the group's response among named social positions, the dealer as "interrogative" and the blacksmith as "judicial," before compressing women and children into three brief dialect utterances and "and so forth."

#### Inference
Within the social-register role, low-pressure village talk can be rendered as a collective performance: occupational labels, dialect, and clipped quotations distinguish speakers while the narrator's summary keeps the exchange comic and crowded.

#### Jurisdiction
- Dimension: `dialogue-social-friction`
- Role: `social register`

#### Comparison
- variation: `the-invisible-man:xiii:443:8444ba3c5fba` - Marvel retains colloquial repetition under direct threat, but the social field contracts from a group chorus to defensive self-exculpation.

#### Limitation
The passage is narrated group talk rather than a complete turn-by-turn conversation, so it does not establish a general dialogue proportion or a universal treatment of dialect.

### Observation 2

#### Passage
- `the-invisible-man:iv:156:bea3b86ad1a8`

#### Observation
Bunting answers Cuss's panic by repeating "a most remarkable story" twice; the narration inserts suspicious looks, assumed wisdom, gravity, and "judicial emphasis" around the repeated formula.

#### Inference
Within comic friction, humour arises from the mismatch between Cuss's agitated report and Bunting's ceremonious but uninformative response. Repetition exposes social posture without requiring an explicit joke.

#### Jurisdiction
- Dimension: `humour-irony`
- Role: `comic friction`

#### Comparison
- related: `the-invisible-man:iv:154:94c548fd6c33` - Cuss's rising voice, repeated insistence, and broken-off exit supply the panic against which Bunting's judicial composure becomes comic.

#### Limitation
The effect depends on this paired exchange and its narratorial framing; it does not show that repetition or formal speech is intrinsically humorous elsewhere in the witness.

### Observation 3

#### Passage
- `the-invisible-man:ix:342:8ec129f34188`

#### Observation
The invisible speaker's conditional threat breaks off before completion, is reinforced by a physical tap, and is followed by Marvel's long reply of denials, imperatives, repetitions, an aside, and a final promise of compliance.

#### Inference
In sustained dialogue, unequal pressure is carried by turn shape as well as content: the dominant speaker leaves the consequence unstated, while the threatened speaker accumulates clauses and self-corrections in an effort to appease him.

#### Jurisdiction
- Dimension: `syntax-rhythm`
- Role: `sustained dialogue`

#### Comparison
- variation: `the-invisible-man:xiii:444:6e6a04602732` - A later threat is syntactically complete and immediately silences Marvel, resisting any rule that pressure always produces an extended reply.

#### Limitation
This is one coercive exchange between Griffin and Marvel; the contrast identifies local syntactic variation, not a dialogue metric transferable to other roles or witnesses. Its paragraph movement is provisional because the retained transcription may not preserve serial or first-book paragraph boundaries.

### Observation 4

#### Passage
- `the-invisible-man:xiii:444:6e6a04602732`

#### Observation
The threat occupies one short sentence, after which Marvel becomes silent; the narrative transfers his response from speech to puffed cheeks and despairing eyes.

#### Inference
This resistant case shows sustained dialogue collapsing into narrated gesture when coercion succeeds. High pressure can therefore shorten the exchange rather than producing the voluble pleading seen elsewhere.

#### Jurisdiction
- Dimension: `dialogue-social-friction`
- Role: `sustained dialogue`

#### Comparison
- variation: `the-invisible-man:ix:342:8ec129f34188` - The earlier threat prompts a much longer appeasing turn, so the same relationship does not yield a fixed conversational rhythm.

#### Limitation
The observation concerns adjacent turns in a single coercive relationship and cannot be generalised to social-register, comic-friction, or cross-witness dialogue frequencies. Any paragraph-level contrast also remains subject to the serial/book transcription limitation.
