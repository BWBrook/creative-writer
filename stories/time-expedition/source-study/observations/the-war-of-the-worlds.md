# Observation Dossier: The War of the Worlds

## Development state

Status: **development**. This dossier records bounded Tier 2 observations of
catastrophe, landscape observation, and evidence-to-speculation movement. The
entries are candidate observations, not adopted findings.

## Observations

### Observation 1

#### Passage
- `the-war-of-the-worlds:i:10:dc75deedfa61`

#### Observation
A reported telescopic blaze and later unexplained markings are followed by the
narrator's qualified retrospective suggestion that the blaze "may have been"
connected with the Martian launch mechanism.

#### Inference
The passage moves from attributed observations to a possible explanation while
keeping the explanatory step grammatically provisional.

#### Jurisdiction
- Dimension: `observation-inference`
- Role: `evidence-to-speculation movement`

#### Comparison
- variation: `the-war-of-the-worlds:i:14:ef804b1ae5e7` - Apparent changes in the planet's size and position are instead corrected as effects of a tired eye.

#### Limitation
Both passages are retrospectively narrated and establish local movements of
thought, not the frequency of this pattern or the historical accuracy of the
astronomy.

#### Candidate claims
- `qualified-speculative-inference`

### Observation 2

#### Passage
- `the-war-of-the-worlds:ii:27:b70ea6d8ec6a`

#### Observation
Ogilvy records the object's size, shape, surface, and heat, but initially
attributes the sound within it to unequal cooling because hollowness has not
yet occurred to him.

#### Inference
This passage records one narrow initial attribution: unequal cooling explains
the sound because hollowness has not yet occurred to Ogilvy. It does not itself
show the later revision or its trigger.

#### Jurisdiction
- Dimension: `observation-inference`
- Role: `evidence-to-speculation movement`

#### Comparison
- related: `the-war-of-the-worlds:ii:33:8084c821ebdd` - Ogilvy later makes a rapid inferential link between the object and the flash on Mars.

#### Limitation
The development index does not contain the intervening source ordinals, so
these records bracket a revision but do not expose its complete trigger or
sequence.

#### Candidate claims
- `qualified-speculative-inference`

### Observation 3

#### Passage
- `the-war-of-the-worlds:v:101:8debc943c595`

#### Observation
The altered common is rendered through a restricted succession of visible
contrasts: pale roads, black tree-tops and roofs, isolated smoke and glow, and
distant spires of flame.

#### Inference
The passage organises the aftermath through selected spatial and colour details
rather than an exhaustive inventory of damage or an explicit explanatory
inference.

#### Jurisdiction
- Dimension: `diction-description`
- Role: `landscape observation`

#### Comparison
- related: `the-war-of-the-worlds:v:94:a8c8d2a3d69e` - The common is compressed into blue sky, brown distance, and black pines as the green light changes their apparent brightness.

#### Limitation
The comparison is local to one setting and one episode; the longer aftermath
passage also carries more descriptive items than the momentary light contrast.

#### Candidate claims
- `selective-observed-description`

### Observation 4

#### Passage
- `the-war-of-the-worlds:v:98:0fa76c4fe402`

#### Observation
The narrator sees a man fall and vegetation ignite before he recognises the
phenomenon as death moving through the crowd.

#### Inference
Catastrophic danger is disclosed through observed consequences that
temporarily outrun the witness's interpretation.

#### Jurisdiction
- Dimension: `suspense-scene-pressure`
- Role: `catastrophe`

#### Comparison
- related: `the-war-of-the-worlds:v:99:fac4a552deba` - Previously tested as possible resistance, this adjacent passage instead extends the same mechanism: flashing bushes, fire, and a suddenly silenced horse disclose the otherwise invisible danger through consequences.

#### Limitation
The two passages are consecutive parts of one event rather than independent
instances, and both are shaped by retrospective narration.

#### Candidate claims
- `danger-through-observation`
