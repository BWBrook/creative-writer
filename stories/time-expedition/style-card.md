# Style Card: Wellsian Time Expedition

Operational companion to [`style-guide.md`](style-guide.md) and
[`style-profile.yml`](style-profile.yml), profile `wells-1895-1899` version
`1.1.0`. This card is a desk reference, not an imitation prompt or phrase bank.

## Authority

1. *The Time Machine*: sole continuity and primary frame, Traveller, and voice
   authority.
2. Tier 2 Wells fiction: bounded role comparison only.
3. Tier 3 Wells nonfiction: bounded explanatory and period comparison, never
   fictional voice or continuity.
4. Tier 4 controls: negative comparison only, never positive guidance.
5. Ledger limits govern every rule. Shared or qualified evidence stays shared
   or qualified.

## Role reminders

- `frame`: keep the chain of witness, telling, record, and judgement legible
  (`practice-mediated-testimony`).
- `reported-account`: keep remembered experience, present telling, and later
  judgement distinct when they shift (`practice-role-shaped-distance`).
- `notebook`: let documentary mediation create meaningful access, omission,
  or uncertainty; do not default to telegraphic diary prose.
- `scientific-inference`: move from selected observation to revisable
  explanation (`practice-qualified-inference`). Qualifiers must do epistemic
  work, not fill a quota.
- `scientific-exposition`: let scene or evidence demand the explanation; use
  analogy, procedure, and classification selectively
  (`practice-qualified-exposition`).
- `social-dialogue`: shape turns through occupation, status, pressure,
  interruption, gesture, and silence (`practice-dialogue-pressure`). Let humour
  arise from functional social friction when the scene supports it.
- `danger-action`: reveal changing danger through observed consequences as
  action develops (`practice-danger-through-observation`). This is a review
  question, not a suspense formula.
- `reflection-closure`: close the available record while preserving only the
  uncertainty or implication the evidence sustains
  (`practice-evidentiary-closure`).

## Observation to inference

1. Establish what the narrator can actually observe.
2. Select details that change interpretation or pressure.
3. Form the best period-bounded explanation available.
4. Mark uncertainty where evidence, perception, or causation remains limited.
5. Revise when new evidence requires it.

Do not replace this sequence with exhaustive inventory, habitual hedging, or
modern scientific assurance in period costume.

## High-risk drift

- Generic Victorian costume carrying a passage without narrative function.
- Heroic certainty, expedition motivation, cadence, or register borrowed from
  a neighbouring control author.
- Modern hard-SF knowledge or explanatory confidence unavailable in the late
  1890s.
- Interchangeable exposition in dialogue, detachable quips, uniformly decorous
  speech, or a fixed sentence cadence.
- Generic AI-surface patterns treated as proof rather than separate editorial
  warnings.

Period vocabulary, adventure energy, and technical detail remain legitimate
when their narrative function is sound.

## Commands

```bash
make style-reference STORY=time-expedition PACK=<packet-id>
make style-lint STORY=time-expedition [CHAPTER=01-the-obstruction]
```

Study packet method, role, evidentiary sequence, and cadence. Never borrow
distinctive phrases, sentence skeletons, images, or local wording.

AI triage uses `dismiss`, `revise`, `monitor`, and `escalate`. Escalate only
ambiguous decisions with material manuscript or profile impact. AI may close a
chapter review; human approval closes story and anthology acceptance. Findings
never change the profile automatically.
