# Operational Style Guide: Wellsian Time Expedition

## Status and purpose

This guide operationalises `style-profile.yml` version `1.1.0`. Its authority
is the human-approved Phase 2B evidence layer, not recollection of Wells, a
generic Victorian register, or the surface resemblance of a generated passage.

The writing objective is a recovered late-1890s expedition manuscript whose
chain of testimony, observation, inference, and surviving record feels
credible within *The Time Machine*'s narrative world. Historical texture serves
that method. It does not replace it. Preserve productive period-bounded error
and uncertainty where the story design calls for them; do not give the narrator
modern scientific confidence dressed in antique diction.

This guide supplies review practices, not an imitation prompt or phrase bank.
It does not authorise copying source wording, reproducing sentence skeletons,
or treating a diagnostic as an authenticity verdict.

## Authority hierarchy

1. *The Time Machine* is Tier 1, the sole story-world canon, and the primary
   voice, frame, and Traveller authority.
2. Selected Tier 2 Wells fiction supplies bounded comparison in its admitted
   roles. It cannot add continuity or override *The Time Machine*.
3. Tier 3 Wells nonfiction supplies bounded comparison for explanation,
   classification, humour, observation, and period language. It is not
   fictional continuity or direct narrative-voice authority.
4. Tier 4 neighbouring authors are negative or comparative controls only.
   Their passages never become positive guidance.
5. The ledger and validation record govern every claim. Raw metrics, rejected
   hypotheses, control practices, adaptations, and remembered generalisations
   carry no positive authority.

Phase 2B retained six `validated` and four `qualified` claims. Nine dimensions
were classified `wells_characteristic_but_shared`; only
`short-form-evidentiary-closure` is `role_specific`. None is strong-confidence.
Shared practice can be historically apt and useful without distinguishing
Wells from neighbouring prose.

## Role guidance

Chapter roles are coarse declarations, not paragraph classifiers. A chapter
may carry several roles, and local passages may move among them.

### Frame (`frame`)

Make the route from event to reader legible: a witness speaks, a listener
judges, a notebook preserves, or a record remains incomplete. Keep credibility
and uncertainty available to the scene rather than announcing an extraordinary
fact from an unmediated authorial position.

- Review with `practice-mediated-testimony`, backed by
  `mediated-testimony-frame` (`validated`, moderate, shared).
- At the ending, use `practice-evidentiary-closure` only within its eligible
  frame and short-form roles.
- Treat visible mediation as a bounded option, not a requirement for all Wells
  or all period fiction.
- Use `frame-and-testimony` and `reflection-and-closure` for comparison.

### Reported account (`reported-account`)

Keep the relation among experienced event, remembered event, present telling,
and later judgement intelligible. Distance may tighten during experience and
reopen when the Traveller reflects, corrects, or addresses his listeners.

- Review with `practice-role-shaped-distance`, backed by
  `role-shaped-narrator-distance` (`validated`, moderate, shared).
- Read `diagnostic-role-shaped-syntax` as local context. It reports approximate
  sentence and paragraph distributions and defines no target cadence.
- Let event pressure change local movement. Do not mechanically alternate long
  and short sentences or force a continuous retrospective detachment.
- Use `traveller-account`; its Moreau passage is bounded authentic variation,
  not continuity or a replacement voice.

### Notebook (`notebook`)

Let documentary form change access to the expedition. A notebook can condense,
omit, preserve immediate uncertainty, or acquire a second layer when another
person reads it. Keep the relation between record, original witness, reader,
and later judgement clear.

- Apply both `practice-mediated-testimony` and
  `practice-role-shaped-distance`; the linked claims remain bounded to their
  approved roles and witnesses.
- Use `diagnostic-role-shaped-syntax` to inspect a sustained local pattern, not
  to manufacture telegraphic diary prose.
- Use `frame-and-testimony` and `traveller-account`. The latter deliberately
  has no Tier 4 control because it compares admitted Wells variation.

### Scientific inference (`scientific-inference`)

Begin from selected observed particulars. Let the narrator construct an
explanation that can remain provisional, meet contrary evidence, or be revised.
Qualification should mark a real limit in knowledge, perception, or causation.

- Review with `practice-qualified-inference`, backed by
  `qualified-speculative-inference` (`validated`, moderate, shared).
- Use `locator-qualified-inference` to find epistemic markers, then decide
  whether each does genuine work. The locator is not a quota for hedging.
- Apply `practice-selective-description` where observation supports inference;
  `selective-observed-description` is `qualified`, tentative, and shared.
- Use `observation-and-exposition` to compare revision, observation, and
  authentic variation.

### Scientific exposition (`scientific-exposition`)

Let explanation answer pressure already created by scene, evidence, dispute,
or practical need. Use analogy, procedure, comparison, and classification
selectively. Keep the narrator's knowledge within the late 1890s and preserve
uncertainty where the evidence cannot close it.

- Review with `practice-qualified-exposition`, backed by
  `qualified-scientific-exposition` (`qualified`, moderate, shared).
- Apply `practice-selective-description` to keep detail organised around an
  act of observation or classification rather than a complete inventory.
- The Tier 3 biology entry demonstrates explanatory-method variation only. Do
  not convert textbook syntax into fictional voice or import later science.
- Technical clarity is legitimate. Expository completeness and modern
  certainty are separate risks.
- Use `observation-and-exposition`.

### Social dialogue (`social-dialogue`)

Shape speech through role, occupation, status, disagreement, danger, and what
each speaker can admit. Let gesture, interruption, silence, summary, and uneven
turn length share the work with quoted speech.

- Review with `practice-dialogue-pressure`, backed by
  `dialogue-pressure-varies-by-context` (`validated`, moderate, shared).
- `diagnostic-dialogue-context` reports an approximate quoted-speech
  proportion after 200 words. It has no automatic high or low threshold.
- `diagnostic-role-shaped-syntax` reports context and no target rhythm.
- For humour, use `practice-humour-social-friction`, backed by
  `humour-through-social-friction` (`validated`, moderate, shared). Let comedy
  arise from incompatible understandings, manners, or status where the scene
  supports it; do not insert detachable quips.
- Do not polish every speaker into a modern idea of Victorian decorum. Social
  difference should remain functional rather than ornamental.
- Use `dialogue-and-social-friction`.

### Danger and action (`danger-action`)

Let danger become legible through observed consequence and altered
interpretation before or while action accelerates. Select the details that
change what the Traveller understands and must do.

- Review with `practice-danger-through-observation`, backed by
  `danger-through-observation` (`qualified`, tentative, shared).
- Apply `practice-selective-description` to landscape and biological detail.
- The approved examples vary from immediate pursuit to catastrophic aftermath.
  The sequence is a question for review, not a formula requiring delayed
  explanation in every danger scene.
- Use `danger-and-action`; its held-out Sea Raiders passage preserves advance
  framing as authentic limiting variation.

### Reflection and closure (`reflection-closure`)

Close the available record with a final evidentiary fact, residue, changed
custody, or altered uncertainty. Allow larger implications to remain active
without adding a thematic explanation or a compulsory emotional seal.

- Review with `practice-evidentiary-closure`, backed by
  `short-form-evidentiary-closure` (`qualified`, moderate, role-specific).
- The held-out *Time Machine* variation closes local testimony but does not
  include the terminal frame; retain that limitation.
- The Tier 4 journey control settles its principal outcomes. This is one local
  contrast, not proof of Wellsian uniqueness or a ban on practical closure.
- Use `reflection-and-closure`.

## Contextual diagnostics

The claimless diagnostics locate possible drift and carry no Wells evidence
bindings:

- `diagnostic-repeated-phrases` locates recurring three-word components after
  a sufficient story sample. Decide whether they form testimony, motif, or
  reusable filler.
- `diagnostic-repeated-openings` locates recurring three-word paragraph starts
  after eight paragraphs. Decide whether they organise the account or flatten
  its launches.
- `diagnostic-uniform-cadence` reviews a sentence-length coefficient of
  variation below `0.45` after twenty sentences (profile 1.1.0; provisional,
  awaiting a Wells measurement by the same scanner). The threshold is broad,
  parser-dependent, and not a Wells norm.

Sentence boundaries, paragraphing, and quotation spans are approximate. In
particular, the source transcription and the long reported account limit what
whole-witness dialogue or paragraph values can establish. No diagnostic score
measures authenticity.

## Variation and negative comparison

Read a packet as a set, not as a model paragraph. Positive examples show a
bounded mechanism. `authentic_variation` shows another legitimate local
realisation or a limit on the claim. `negative_control` asks whether a broad
feature is shared with a neighbouring author or locally contrasts with the
Wells evidence.

The controls show that mediated testimony, qualified technical explanation,
social-friction humour, and observation-before-recognition can occur outside
Wells. The journey-ending control supplies one local non-match. None grants a
control author continuity, positive voice authority, or phrase-borrowing
permission.

## Drift risks

### Generic Victorian costume

Use `anti-drift-generic-victorian-costume` when antique diction, manners, or
social furniture appear to carry the passage without an evidentiary or dramatic
function. Period vocabulary is legitimate when it performs real narrative
work; age alone is neither proof nor defect.

### Neighbouring adventure voice

Use `anti-drift-neighbouring-adventure-voice` when heroic certainty, expedition
motivation, danger cadence, or technical register seems borrowed from a Tier 4
control. Adventure energy is legitimate. The control exists to test convention,
not to prescribe voice.

### Modern hard-SF explanation

Use `anti-drift-modern-hard-sf-explanation` when explanation depends on modern
knowledge or assurance unavailable to the late-1890s narrator. Technical detail
is legitimate when historically bounded and dramatically functional. Phase 2B
admitted no modern hard-SF control, so this manual rule has no source locator.

### Generic AI surface

Generic AI-writing findings remain a separate editorial surface. Em-dash and
contrastive-parallelism warnings also remain surface warnings. They cannot
support Wells claims, overrule authentic evidence, prove AI authorship, enter a
combined score, or trigger an automatic rewrite.

## Packet workflow

Compile a deliberate source packet before drafting or reviewing its role:

```bash
make style-reference STORY=time-expedition PACK=<packet-id>
```

Use this instruction for every excerpt:

> Study narrative method, evidentiary sequence, role, and cadence. Do not
> borrow distinctive phrases, sentence skeletons, images, or local wording.

Inspect the focal passage with its bounded context, selection rationale,
authority tier, claim status, scope, and prohibited overgeneralisation. Return
to the source only to understand function, never to harvest reusable language.

## Review workflow

Run the whole story by default or target one chapter during a drafting pass:

```bash
make style-lint STORY=time-expedition
make style-lint STORY=time-expedition CHAPTER=01-the-obstruction
```

The AI reviews every finding in manuscript context and assigns one provisional
disposition:

- `dismiss`: mechanically true and editorially irrelevant;
- `revise`: a clear local prose or drift problem;
- `monitor`: harmless locally but worth watching across the manuscript;
- `escalate`: ambiguous and consequential enough for human judgement.

Escalate selectively. A human calibration pack normally contains three to
five passages, one precise question per passage, the intended role, the cited
evidence and variation, the AI judgement, and the decision impact.

Story and anthology acceptance requires explicit human approval. Review
judgements are recorded in review-pack manifests, commit messages, and the
conversation that produced them; there is no separate review-record format.

## Profile discipline

Findings do not mutate the profile. Every adopted profile change must
increment the version, name the affected rule or tolerance, and cite the
motivating evidence in its commit message. One ruling remains calibration
evidence, not a new universal rule.
