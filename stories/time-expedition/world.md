# World Notes

## Canonical literary world

- H. G. Wells's *The Time Machine* is the sole story-world canon and primary
  style anchor.
- H. G. Wells's *The Invisible Man* supports dialogue and social handling only.
- The retained source texts and their declared analytical roles live under
  `original/` and `source-study/`.
- Later adaptation-specific characters, dialogue, machinery, and visual
  designs are excluded.

## Recovered-manuscript priority

The project treats the anthology as a plausible late-1890s Wells manuscript.
The governing order is Wellsian authenticity, period knowledge, internal
scientific coherence, then modern plausibility. A productive Victorian error
may remain when the evidence and reasoning available to the characters support
it.

## Machine continuity

- The machine follows the terrestrial and local crustal frame through time. It
  supplies no independent lateral travel.
- Ancient Richmond is the transformed site beneath the Traveller's study.
- Deep-time targeting is coarse. Shorter forward traverses establish relative
  sequence more securely than an absolute date.
- Each rematerialisation requires occupiable space around the machine.
- Temporal travel does not protect the unattended machine, move it through
  debris, or remove the Traveller's physical fatigue.
- The cause of terrestrial anchoring remains unknown and becomes a Royal
  Society question.
- The obstruction that delayed the Traveller's earlier return remains a
  separate anthology mystery. It does not cause the Permian catastrophe.

## Expedition I setting

Modern backstage placement is near the Permian-Triassic transition, about
251.9 million years ago. One current model places the crustal tract later
beneath Richmond near 17 degrees north. Characters should describe it only as
within or near the northern tropic.

The adopted local landscape is a speculative regional analogue:

- an exposed rocky ridge or scarp;
- a shallow fissure refuge beneath resistant rock;
- an alluvial plain crossed by a substantial seasonal river;
- a terminal saline lake or playa;
- arid-adapted vegetation and sparse animal life.

No terminal-Permian deposits survive at modern Richmond. The river, lake,
refuge, flora, and fauna are story choices rather than recovered local facts.

## Catastrophe model

Siberian Traps volcanism, carbon-cycle disruption, warming, and associated
environmental stress form the modern backstage model. The Traveller can infer
powerful distant volcanic action from indirect local effects. He cannot locate
the source, invoke plate tectonics, or establish global synchrony.

The selected local sequence is restrained: haze and heat, water stress,
aquatic distress, vegetation failure, faunal displacement, hydrological
reversal, flood-assisted undercutting, and refuge collapse. Fire remains
unused by default. Direct Siberian ash, local volcanism, and convenient
earthquakes are excluded.

## Organisms and evidence

- The complete specimen is a 12-20 centimetre palaeoniscid-grade ganoid fish,
  named through period comparison rather than a modern species claim.
- The recurring predator is an unnamed medium-large theriodont-like carnivore
  with a scar across the left shoulder. Its later identity remains an informed
  inference.
- The predator begins indifferent to the Traveller. Hunger, displacement, and
  catastrophe alter its risk behaviour.
- The proof satchel preserves a bounded sample set. Lost material prevents a
  complete causal demonstration.
