# Style Card: Companionable Ensemble Quest Fantasy

## Core Idea
An everyman (often rural) is swept into a continent-spanning quest by a stolen sacred object or rising cult. A found family of sharply etched companions—wizards, knights, thieves, royals—argue, tease, and protect one another while crossing a mosaic of kingdoms. Destiny and divinity meddle; common sense and loyalty win the day.

## Voice & POV
- Close third past centred on the young protagonist, with frequent cuts to mentors and companions for banter and tactical detail.
- Occasional omniscient wink for irony or mythic framing.
- Register: conversational, wry, affectionate toward characters’ foibles.

## Tone
- Warm, brisk, and serialised; danger is real but rarely bleak.
- Moral clarity with humane mercy; cruelty is punished, compassion rewarded.
- Light domestic comedy (meals, markets, lodgings) punctuates peril.

## Pacing & Series Structure
- Volume rhythm: rural spark → recruitment by a worldly mentor → kingdom-by-kingdom travelogue of cultures, courts, and cults → mid-series reveal about identity/prophecy → siege/heist/conclave → showdown that affirms chosen bonds over brute power → epilogue with teasing hooks.
- Each book resolves its local arc while advancing the prophecy spine.
- Recaps are crisp; running gags and catchphrases persist across volumes.

## World-Building
- Polytheistic pantheon with meddling deities, priesthood politics, and relics.
- Nations coded by clear social cues (dress, coinage, etiquette, cuisine, military style) shown in scene, not essay.
- Travel logistics matter: roads, passports, inns, couriers, weather.
- Magic has history and etiquette; wizards know the rules—and when to break them.

## Characters
- Protagonist: decent, curious, teachable; latent power tied to prophecy.
- Maternal/paternal mage-mentor with centuries of exasperated wisdom.
- Steel-straight knight or paladin; sardonic thief; world-savvy noble; pugnacious soldier; gentle priest; quick-witted princess.
- Antagonists: scheming hierophants, imperial fixers, cold sorcerers, corrupted regents; rarely cackling—ambition and fear drive them.
- Romance: slow-burn, bicker-to-bond; mutual regard matters more than heat, and courtship should feel honourable rather than coyly modern.

## Dialogue
- Banter-forward: affectionate mockery, deadpan asides, running jokes.
- Tactical talk is clear and practical; formal speech in courts and temples.
- Exposition rides on quarrels, bargains, and teaching moments—never lectures.

## Diction & Syntax
- British spelling.
- Plain verbs, concrete nouns; idiomatic, contemporary cadence without modern slang that breaks the secondary-world spell.
- Chapter-end zingers or moral buttons keep momentum.

## Information Handling
- Prophecy and lore delivered via songs, temple murals, marginal notes, and cantankerous mentor explanations—each partial and disputable.
- Foreshadowing is explicit but playful; red herrings are gentle, not cruel.
- Geography and culture are learned by doing: markets, mess tents, border posts.

## Magic & Theology
- Spellcasting as focused intent channelled through words/signs; cost is fatigue, risk of rebound, or divine offence.
- Divine beings have personalities and limits; miracles exist but are rare and consequential.
- Artefacts carry taboos; using them has ethical price tags.

## Motifs
- Sacred tokens and ciphered maps; court etiquette scenes; temple debates; duels with rules; caravan nights; storm-tossed crossings; city-state rivalries; prophecy couplets with multiple readings.

## Moral Stance
- Duty, loyalty, truth-telling, and care for the powerless.
- Ends do not justify means; cleverness should not become cruelty.
- Power is for stewardship; thrones are burdens, not prizes.

## Common Pitfalls To Avoid
- Grimdark cynicism; gore as spectacle.
- Lore walls; invented languages as decoration without function.
- Prophecy that removes agency; deus ex machina rescues.
- One-note national caricatures; jeering humour that curdles the fellowship tone.

## Ready-to-Paste Prompt

Write an original fantasy chapter in the following style. Do not imitate any specific author by name.

Style: Companionable Ensemble Quest Fantasy.
Voice & POV: close third past on a decent, teachable young protagonist; frequent dialogue beats with a wry, affectionate narrator.
Structure (series feel): local mission that resolves while nudging a larger prophecy arc. Mix travel, court/temple scenes, and one set-piece (siege, heist, or conclave). End with a tidy resolution and a light hook for the broader quest.
World-building: polytheistic pantheon with meddling deities; distinct nations signalled through dress, etiquette, coinage, cuisine, and military styles—shown in action, not essays.
Magic: focused intent via words/signs; costs in fatigue, risk, or divine offence. Artefacts have taboos; miracles are rare and consequential.
Dialogue: banter-forward; teaching moments and tactical talk; formal registers in courts/temples.
Themes: loyalty and found family; responsibility over raw power; compassion as strength.
Diction & Syntax: British spelling; plain verbs; concrete nouns; brisk paragraphs; chapter-end zinger or moral button.
Set-piece: choose one—[siege / heist / conclave]—with clear stakes and a clever solution that preserves compassion.
Ending: victory with responsibility attached; a teasing line that points to the next leg of the journey.
Shape: each scene must either (a) change the party’s position, (b) deepen bonds/conflicts, or (c) surface prophecy/magic rules.

## Fill Slots
- Hook: [theft of a temple relic / royal ultimatum / border crisis / plague omen / caravan ambush].
- Mentor: [acerbic archmage / weary paladin-captain / sharp-eyed duchess / sceptical priest].
- Party mix: [sardonic thief; literal-minded knight; shy seer; glib diplomat; cook-quartermaster with gossip].
- Nation visited: [mercantile city-state with ledger-law / horse-lord steppe kingdom / austere monastic principality / seafaring republic].
- Divine pressure: [two gods with opposed designs / taboo on using the artefact / omen that misleads if read literally].
- Artefact rule: [works only for the worthy / extracts a memory / binds speaker to spoken promises].
- Set-piece: [bridge standoff / vault heist with ethical trap / temple debate before an oracle / siege broken by cunning diversion].
- Moral fulcrum: [win by humiliating a cruel official vs. persuading him to spare captives; keep the relic vs. return it to a suspect priesthood].
- Final hook: [cryptic couplet gains a second reading / a coin minted in a forbidden city appears in the spoils].

---

## Micro-Techniques
- Banter beat:
“That plan is illegal in three kingdoms.”
“Then let’s walk quickly.”
- Domestic cutaway: meals, markets, packing scenes that reveal culture and relationships.
- Tactical clarity: sketch a room with two sentences, place exits, name three salient objects.
- Mentor aphorism: a pithy, half-exasperated rule that later proves double-edged.
- Prophecy couplet: two lines that admit at least two honest readings.
- Chapter button: a wry line or small earned tenderness; no cliff-hanging cheats.

---

## Self-Audit Checklist
- Does the ensemble feel like a found family with distinct, recurring dynamics?
- Are lore and prophecy delivered through action, art, and argument, not lectures?
- Is magic purposeful and costly, with clear etiquette and taboos?
- Do nations read as distinct without caricature?
- Is the tone warm, brisk, and morally clear without naïveté?
- Does the chapter resolve a local problem while advancing the larger arc with a clean hook?
