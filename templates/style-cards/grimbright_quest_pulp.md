# Style Card: Grimbright Quest Pulp

## Core Idea
Fast, vivid dungeon-romp fantasy: rooms, traps, beasts, puzzles, and shiny loot—told with immediacy, clear stakes, and a game-table sense of consequence. The world is perilous but playful; courage and low cunning matter.

## Voice & POV
- Default: second-person present for arcade immediacy (“You duck. Steel sings.”).
- Alternative: close third past with imperative undertones (“He checks the lintel; do not trust lintels.”).
- Narrator is brisk, practical, occasionally sardonic; never omniscient.

## Tone
- High-energy, grim yet gleeful; danger is real but not nihilistic.
- Tension releases via dry asides or gallows humour.
- Wonder arises from tactile detail—engravings, odours, glints, textures.

## Pacing & Structure
- Snap entry (tavern hook or threshold crossed) → string of discrete encounters → mid-dungeon reversal → set-piece gauntlet → boss confrontation → short, ironic epilogue.
- Encounters are modular; each features a clear risk, a tell, and a payoff.
- Resource pressure is constant (time, light, stamina, gear).

## World-Building
- Patchwork sandbox: taverns, hedge-markets, guilds, ruined keeps, catacombs, fungal warrens, brine caverns.
- Cultures sketched with strong signals (insignia, coinage, oaths) not essays.
- Magic is practical and trap-adjacent: glyphs, mirrors, binding circles, hungry doors.

## Characters
- Protagonist: capable, opportunistic; defined by kit, scars, and a simple code (keep bargains, pay debts, no cruelty to bystanders).
- Allies: mercenary priest, nimble cutpurse, grim sellsword, cheery hedge-mage—each with a trick and a price.
- Antagonists: tyrant stewards, cult hierophants, eldritch custodians, mercenary bands.
- Monsters have signatures (scent, sound, silhouette) and a twist; avoid bestiary clichés unless you freshen them.

## Dialogue
- Short, transactional, with prices, threats, and grudging respect.
- Rumours and warnings replace exposition dumps.
- Oaths and boasts carry local colour; avoid modern slang.

## Diction & Syntax
- British spelling.
- Noun-verb forward: “Hook, wrench, kick. The lock yields.”
- Use enumerations for kit and loot; concrete, tactile nouns (hemp, pitch, chalk, iron, bone, mildew).
- Occasional imperative aside to the reader/hero (“Check the ceiling.”).

## Information Handling
- Telegraph danger with sensory tells: draft from a crack, iron tang, scratch-marks at ankle height.
- Clues are layered: an obvious hint, a subtler sign, a payoff for attention.
- Keep mechanics implied: talk of risk, leverage, fatigue—no rule sheets.

## Encounter Design Heuristics
- Each scene changes one of: position, resources, allies, knowledge.
- Every trap has a fair tell and at least two solutions (tool use, caution, bargain).
- Loot is specific and story-useful (keys, maps, tonics, tokens), not generic coin-heaps.

## Motifs
- Numbered doors; brass gongs; bone dice; green verdigris; mildew and myrrh; chalk sigils; fake treasure on a pressure plate; captive spirits in glass; oath-coins bitten at the bar.

## Moral Stance
- Pragmatic honour: don’t cheat the desperate; do cheat the cruel.
- Greed tempts; mercy pays off later.
- Power has upkeep; trophies have curses.

## Common Pitfalls To Avoid
- Lore monologues; balanced “magic systems” explained like textbooks.
- Edgelord cruelty; sexualised peril; slapstick invulnerability.
- Endless corridors with no decisions; boss fights without a puzzle.

## Ready-to-Paste Prompt

Write an original fantasy adventure in the following style. Do not imitate any specific author by name.

Style: Grimbright Quest Pulp.
Voice & POV: [second-person present / close third past]; brisk, tactile, slightly sardonic; no omniscience.
Structure: threshold hook → modular encounters (each with tell, risk, payoff) → mid-dungeon reversal → set-piece gauntlet → boss confrontation → curt, ironic epilogue.
Focus: traps with fair tells, monsters with signatures, resource pressure (time, light, stamina, kit), and meaningful loot.
Dialogue: short, transactional; rumours over lectures; prices, oaths, bargains.
Diction & Syntax: British spelling; concrete nouns; verb-driven lines; enumerated gear/loot. Use occasional imperative asides (“Check the lintel.”).
Information Handling: layer clues; hint solutions without spelling them out; keep “mechanics” implied via risk/fatigue/consequence.
Themes: courage and low cunning; pragmatic honour; mercy that later pays.
Ending: earned escape or victory with a sting (spent resources, cursed trophy, new map fragment).
Shape: each encounter must alter position, resources, allies, or knowledge.
Guardrails: no cruelty to bystanders; avoid gratuitous gore or sexualised peril; no lore essays; keep the fun-to-danger ratio taut.

## Fill Slots
- Hook: [oath-debt / bounty writ / lost caravan charter / blackmail note / plague cure rumour].
- Locale: [collapsed aqueduct under a hill-fort / brine-washed sea caves / fungal catacombs below a market / desert step-well / glass-roofed arboretum in ruins].
- Ally: [knife-quick cutpurse / lantern-proud priest / hedge-mage with binding chalk / taciturn sellsword].
- Signature monster: [echo-mimic wight / blind beetle-knight / moss-laden strangler / mirror-jackal / stitched homunculus].
- Trap with fair tell: [mercury level in a relief’s eye / slack chain over a pit / cold draft through keyhole / soot halo on a flagstone].
- Puzzle: [rotating sigil-discs / counterweight sluice / coin-oath that opens a reliquary / sun-angle through coloured glass].
- Loot (story-useful): [salt-sealed tonic / map shard on vellum / oath-coin stamped with a lion / star-metal nail / whispering mirror shard].
- Boss: [cult hierophant with a living veil / tidebound revenant / golem that learns / tyrant steward with chained ghosts].
- Moral fulcrum: [free captives or take the idol / keep the map or pay a debt / spare the monster that guards orphans].
- Final sting: [cursed token warms in your palm / rival claims your bounty / the map fragment completes a skull-shaped coastline].

## Micro-Techniques
- Enumerated kit: “Pack: hemp rope, iron spikes, chalk, two tonics, a brass whistle.”
- Tell sentence: “Cold breath slips from the keyhole.”
- Action tricolon: “Hook, heave, slip.”
- Room header cadence: “The Copper Gallery: green-streaked statues, a sunken floor, and a whisper that isn’t wind.”
- Rumour line: “Old Talla swears the third bell is for the dead, not the hour.”
- Fair bargain: “A name, a coin, and you don’t look back.”

---

## Self-Audit Checklist
- Does every encounter change position, resources, allies, or knowledge?
- Are traps fairly telegraphed with sensory tells and multiple solutions?
- Is the prose verb-driven, concrete, and light on lore?
- Do monsters have a signature and a twist?
- Is resource pressure maintained without dice jargon?
- Does the ending land with reward and consequence, not just fireworks?
