# Style Card: Haunted Justice Whodunnit

## Core Idea
A restrained mystery of law, guilt, and haunting: practical investigators follow material clues through a troubled community until a crime, older than its first appearance, becomes morally unavoidable. The supernatural is not spectacle; it is witness, pressure, and consequence.

## Voice & POV
- Default: close third past, anchored on one capable investigator or a small company with a clear working lens.
- Register: plain, observant, humane, and sceptical; reverent toward oath, office, and the dead without becoming pious.
- The narration should notice work, weather, fatigue, objects, and custom before emotion.

## Tone
- Grave but not bleak; haunted but not hysterical.
- Suspense grows from evidence, silence, obligation, and the narrowing of choices.
- Moral weight sits in small actions: a door left locked, a body recovered, a lie repeated under pressure.

## Pacing & Structure
- Public disorder or practical failure opens the story.
- Inquiry moves through social permission, place-based search, documentary or ritual clues, descent into danger, confrontation, and public or ritual resolution.
- Each scene should change knowledge, leverage, danger, or moral risk.
- End scenes on an image, task, or withheld answer rather than a thesis.

## World-Building
- Use strong, specific material signals: tavern boards, seal-cords, brass plates, tallow smoke, salt wind, wet stone, old wax, mud on boots.
- Institutions matter: household, guard, temple, dock, law, family reputation, and debt.
- Magic should feel local and juridical: bindings, shrines, old rites, oaths, curses, relics, and signs.
- History appears through scars in buildings and habits, not lecture.

## Characters
- Protagonists are competent adults whose virtues are attention, restraint, and keeping promises.
- Witnesses and servants have agency; they know rooms, timings, keys, sounds, and daily deviations.
- Antagonists are driven by greed, fear, pride, debt, or cowardice rather than pure villainy.
- The dead may be silent, but they are not decorative. Their claims structure the story.

## Dialogue
- Short, courteous when useful, exact when pressure rises.
- Questions seek custom, sequence, motive, and contradiction.
- Let evasions matter. A poor answer can be more revealing than a confession.
- Avoid modern slang and therapy-language.

## Diction & Syntax
- British spelling.
- Concrete nouns and literal verbs. Use adjectives sparingly.
- Permit gentle archaism through word choice and social form, not faux-antique grammar.
- Sentences should generally be clean and controlled, with occasional longer lines for weather, crowd, or ritual pressure.

## Information Handling
- Clues should be layered: public rumour, physical trace, witness correction, documentary or ritual key, final contradiction.
- Supernatural information should arrive through disturbance, arrangement, repetition, refusal, or bodily consequence.
- Do not explain feelings when an object, silence, wound, or task can carry them.
- Keep at least one honest unknown.

## Action & Risk
- Violence should be brief, costly, and physically legible.
- Fatigue, cold, darkness, injury, noise, and bad footing persist across scenes.
- Combat is most interesting when it alters evidence, trust, position, light, or the chance of justice.

## Motifs
Darkened beacons; ferries in bad weather; keys and locked rooms; salt on stone; shrine dust; journal margins; brass scales; cut rope; wet steps; old bones; a body that must be carried home; light restored after judgment.

## Moral Stance
- Truth before convenience; mercy after truth, not instead of it.
- Law is necessary but imperfect. Vengeance is tempting but not identical with justice.
- The work of the living is to make the dead properly known.

## Common Pitfalls To Avoid
- Dungeon-room transcription without narrative selection.
- Lore essays, boxed text, or rules jargon.
- Melodramatic haunting, quip-heavy banter, or ornamental grimness.
- Modernising older social worlds until their obligations and cruelties lose force.
- Solving mystery by sudden revelation instead of accumulated evidence.

## Ready-to-Paste Prompt

Write an original haunted mystery adventure in the following style. Do not imitate any specific author by name.

Style: Haunted Justice Whodunnit.
Voice & POV: close third past, anchored on a capable investigator or small company; plain, observant, restrained, humane.
Structure: public disorder or practical failure -> permission and inquiry -> place-based search -> ritual/documentary clues -> descent into danger -> confrontation with the lie -> justice or failed justice -> quiet coda.
Evidence: public rumour, physical traces, witness correction, written records, ritual signs, and final contradiction.
Supernatural: witness and pressure, not spectacle; signs arrive through moved objects, extinguished light, cold, repeated sounds, and moral consequence.
Dialogue: short, exact, period-aware; no modern slang; evasions matter.
Diction & Syntax: British spelling; concrete nouns; literal verbs; sparse modifiers; gentle archaism by lexis only.
Themes: law and mercy; guilt under pressure; the dead demanding recognition; institutions protecting themselves until evidence forces them open.
Ending: resolve the central moral question with an action, image, or ritual change rather than a speech.
Guardrails: no rules jargon, no boxed-text feel, no lore dumps, no cheap twist, no sentimental ghost theatrics.

## Fill Slots
- Public failure: [dark beacon / missing bell / poisoned well / locked gate / vanished tithe cart].
- Investigator lens: [retired sergeant / household clerk / abbey physician / ferryman's daughter / wandering oath-sworn].
- Institution under pressure: [noble house / city watch / abbey / dock guild / border court].
- Silent witness: [bound spirit / hidden journal / reliquary object / corpse with an impossible wound].
- Physical clue: [cut rope / wrong wax / salt mud / missing key / torn seal-cord].
- Moral fulcrum: [trial vs. private punishment / recover the body vs. loot the shrine / protect order vs. speak truth].
- Final image: [beacon relit / bell rung at dawn / body carried over the causeway / sealed door opened in daylight].

---

## Micro-Techniques
- Task anchor: begin scenes with work being done: rowing, searching, washing, carrying, unlocking, lighting, binding.
- Witness pressure: let the ghost correct the living by moving a small object one inch too far.
- Legal texture: names, seals, keys, testimony, custody, debt, permission, and rank should alter what people can say.
- Object as feeling: a cup not drunk, a glove left on a desk, a candle burning out, a rope retied.
- Evidence ladder: rumour -> trace -> document -> body -> lie under pressure.

---

## Self-Audit Checklist
- Does every scene change knowledge, leverage, danger, or moral risk?
- Are supernatural moments restrained and evidentiary?
- Does the prose carry emotion through task, object, weather, and consequence?
- Is the mystery solved by accumulated evidence rather than authorial surprise?
- Do institutions and lower-status witnesses both have agency?
- Does justice cost something, even when it is the right answer?
