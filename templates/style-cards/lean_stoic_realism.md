# Style Card: Lean Stoic Realism

## Core Idea
Emotion is carried by work, weather, objects, and consequence—not by explanation. A capable person does a concrete task under strain; meaning emerges from what is done, not said.

## Voice & POV
- First-person past or close third past.
- Linear time with clean, factual transitions (“In the morning…”, “Later we…”, “At dusk…”).
- Narrative distance is tight; thought is brief and practical, not essayistic.

## Tone
- Restrained, unsentimental, humane.
- Pain, love, fear, and pride are shown through choices, fatigue, and small mercies.
- No melodrama; irony is dry, not sneering.

## Pacing & Structure
- Anchor every scene in a physical task: mending, packing, stalking, running a boat, setting a line, walking a ridge, cleaning a wound, cooking, fixing an engine.
- Escalation comes from weather, injury, scarcity, time, or a promise coming due.
- End scenes on an image or action beat, not a thesis. Endings land with earned simplicity.

## Setting / World Detail
- Real places or lightly veiled ones; name rivers, bays, streets, mountains, train lines.
- Weather and light do narrative work (wind direction, glare, cold in the hands).
- Use prices, weights, calibres, species, brands/tools only as they are used. No brochures.
- Politics and history appear as pressures (rationing, closures, strikes, war orders), not lectures.

## Characters
- Working adults who know a craft; dignity in competence.
- Supporting figures should be defined by craft, judgement, and local knowledge, not decorative roles.
- Antagonism comes from pride, cowardice, hunger, or bad bargains, not moustache-twirling.

## Dialogue
- Short, unornamented exchanges that move the task or test a bond.
- Tags are plain (“he said”, “she said”); adverbs are rare.
- Subtext over confession; what is not answered matters.

## Diction & Syntax
- British spelling.
- Concrete nouns and strong, literal verbs. Few adjectives; fewer adverbs.
- Parataxis and coordination (“and… and…”) for march and pressure.
- Sentences mostly 8–18 words, with an occasional longer one to carry a coastline, a sky, or a crowd.

## Information Handling
- Do not explain feelings; let objects and actions bear them (a glass not drunk, a chair left empty, a knot retied twice).
- Backstory appears in one or two matter-of-fact lines when the task forces it.
- Keep at least one honest unknown.

## Action & Risk
- Physics obeyed: mass, distance, cold, blood loss, recoil, fuel, tide.
- Injury and fatigue persist across scenes.
- Courage is keeping the bargain and finishing the work.

## Motifs
- Dawn wind on water; dust in the mouth; bottle on a sill; a map with folds; a letter folded again; a fish’s weight; the smell of cordite or diesel; snow taking prints; the sound of boots in a corridor.

## Moral Stance
- Tell the truth, pay your debts, don’t brag.
- Mercy when you can afford it; clean ruthlessness when you can’t.
- Honour is work done right under pressure.

## Common Pitfalls To Avoid
- Purple metaphor, therapy dialogue, quipstorms.
- Over-wrought interiority; omniscient moralising.
- Genre pyrotechnics that violate physics.
- Caricatured machismo; synthetic toughness mistaken for moral weight.

## Ready-to-Paste Prompt

Write an original short story in the following style. Do not imitate any specific author by name.

Style: Lean Stoic Realism.
Voice & POV: [first-person past / close third past]; tight, factual, non-ornate.
Scenes: each anchored in a concrete task ([state tasks]). Escalation via weather, injury, scarcity, time, or a promise coming due.
Dialogue: brief, plain, subtextual; tags like “he said / she said”; no adverbial flourishes.
Diction & Syntax: British spelling; concrete nouns; strong literal verbs; minimal modifiers; coordinated “and…and” chains for pressure; sentences mostly short with an occasional earned long line for landscape or crowd.
Information Handling: no explicit statements of emotion; show feeling through objects, choice, and consequence; give one or two lines of backstory only when the task forces it; leave one honest unknown.
World detail: name places, weather, light, prices/weights only as used; physics obeyed; fatigue and injury persist.
Themes: dignity in work, cost of promises, small mercies.
Ending: close on an image or simple action that carries the weight (no speeches).
Shape: each scene must change task status, resources, or relationship tension.
Do not include: purple prose, melodrama, exclamation marks, miracle saves, lecture-paragraphs, or confessionals.

## Fill Slots
- Task: [run the boat through a squall / dress a field wound / sell a rifle to pay a debt / pack a house after a death / stalk feral deer on a burnt ridge / fix a seized net winch].
- Locale & season: [Bass Strait in a hard southerly / inland river in drought / winter city at closing time / alpine hut in thaw].
- Companion tension: [ex-partner / younger sibling / old friend / rival / cautious guide].
- Scar or limit: [bad knee / drink not touched / failing eye / old shrapnel / seasickness].
- Object motif: [letter / knife / empty chair / bottle / old map / watch].
- Ethical hinge: [keep a promise vs. take the safer job / spare a wounded animal vs. finish it / tell a hard truth vs. keep the team together].
- Final image: [sun on a flat sea after the blow / a plate set for one less / blood washed from a deck / tracks filling with new snow].

---

## Micro-Techniques
- Paratactic pressure: “We tightened the lashings and the wind came up and the spray went cold in the face.”
- Objective correlative: “He set the glass down untouched and folded the letter again.”
- Factual pain: “The hand shook after and would not stop.”
- Inventory line: “Pack: rope, tarpaulin, two tins, the old knife.”
- Price anchor: “The fuel was dear and the barometer was falling.”
- Earned long sentence (landscape): one unbroken line to carry a coast, ridge, or crowd moving as weather does.

---

## Self-Audit Checklist
- Does every scene centre on a concrete task with consequence?
- Are feelings shown by choice, object, weather, and wound rather than declared?
- Do physics, fatigue, and price shape action?
- Is the prose mostly noun-verb with minimal modifiers?
- Do dialogue beats move work or test bonds without speechifying?
- Does the ending close on an image that earns its weight?
