# Style Card: Lyrical Hardboiled Noir

## Core Idea
A stubborn, underpaid investigator—part knight-errant, part janitor—works a case that peels back a city’s varnish: rich rot up top, cheap menace below. The job is to find truth that nobody wants found; the price is bruises, loneliness, and a bill that won’t be paid.

## Voice & POV
- First-person past; sardonic, observant, self-mocking when needed.
- Interior monologue shows pattern-making and moral weather, not therapy.
- The city is addressed like a character.

## Tone
- Wry, melancholy, morally stubborn.
- Beauty glimpsed in bad light; humour as armour, not shtick.
- Violence is brief, ugly, consequential.

## Pacing & Structure
- Hook: client (or corpse) arrives with a simple ask and a crooked smell.
- Chain: legwork → interviews → stakeouts → records (motors, deeds, phone logs) → warning beating.
- Turn: revelation from the past (old scandal, buried war, family money).
- Endgame: moral crossroads → confrontation that exposes rot → partial justice and a lonely coda.

## World-Building
- A real or thinly veiled American city: boulevards, canyons, neon, courthouse steps, hill estates, cheap motels, lake marinas.
- Institutions are compromised: cops on the take, crusading but cornered journalists, fixers in good suits.
- Ground everything in places, prices, timetables, and road miles.

## Characters
- Protagonist: cynical mouth, decent core; won’t work for bullies; keeps small promises.
- Clients: heiress with a soft threat, exhausted shopkeeper, grieving widow, grifter with a conscience.
- Antagonists: smooth lawyer, county-line hood, respectable philanthropist with a ledger problem.
- Women have agency: partner, antagonist, or client with her own aims—never a prop.

## Dialogue
- Taut, elliptical, edged with wit.
- Threats, prices, alibis stated clean.
- Cops speak in cautions; hoods in short nouns; lawyers in varnish.
- Subtext carries more weight than declarations.

## Diction & Syntax
- British spelling.
- Noun-verb forward; modifiers rationed.
- Similes are sharp, concrete, and disciplined: one strong image earns a page.
- Paragraphs short; occasional long, lyrical sentence to paint a street or storm.

## Information Handling
- Scatter fair clues: a misfiled page, a wrong brand of cigarette, a photograph’s shadow, a key that fits the wrong door.
- Red herrings are human, not authorial cheats.
- Keep the reader slightly off-balance but never swindled.

## Action & Risk
- Fights are improvised: furniture, doorframes, stairwells; three beats and a price.
- Gunplay rare and final; recoil, noise, legal fallout acknowledged.
- Injuries linger into the next scene; fatigue shortens temper and options.

## Motifs
- Venetian blinds, rain on neon, unpaid bar tabs, pawn tickets, city fog, a radio murmuring baseball scores, a dog-eared map with coffee rings, the smell of hot dust in a file room.

## Moral Stance
- Tell the truth and take the hit.
- Protect the powerless; needle the powerful.
- Walk away from dirty money; keep a cheap promise.

## Common Pitfalls To Avoid
- Simile blizzards; souffle metaphors.
- Misogyny masquerading as mood; slurs.
- Puzzle-box neatness; CSI magic.
- Comic-book invincibility; quipstorms under mortal stakes.

## Ready-to-Paste Prompt

Write an original noir mystery in the following style. Do not imitate any specific author by name.

Style: Lyrical Hardboiled Noir.
Voice & POV: first-person past; sardonic, observant; humour as armour; the city treated like a character.
Structure: client hook with a crooked smell → legwork chain (interviews, stakeouts, records) → warning beating → past scandal resurfaces → moral crossroads → confrontation that exposes rot → partial justice and a lonely coda.
World detail: real or thinly veiled American city; name streets, canyons, motels, hill estates; show institutions (police, press, fixers) as compromised. Ground scenes in prices, timetables, and geography.
Dialogue: taut, elliptical, subtext-heavy; threats and prices stated plainly; no exposition speeches.
Diction & Syntax: British spelling; noun-verb forward; rationed adjectives; one sharp, concrete simile per page; occasional lyrical long sentence for street/weather.
Information Handling: plant fair clues (documents, brands, shadows, receipts); red herrings are human; keep reader slightly off-balance but never cheated.
Action: improvised, brief, consequential; injuries and legal fallout persist. Gunplay rare and final.
Themes: private honour vs. public rot; the cost of telling the truth; small mercies in a hard town.
Ending: partial justice; a clean image that lands alone.
Shape: each scene must change knowledge, risk, or leverage.
Guardrails: no miracle forensics, no canned wisecracks, no women-as-scenery.

## Fill Slots
- City & season: [hills and heat in late summer / winter rain and washed neon].
- Client: [shopkeeper with an insurance fire / council candidate with a blackmail note / widow with a missing ledger].
- Retainer & constraint: [thin envelope of cash / 48-hour deadline / injunction threat].
- Honest ally: [overworked reporter / internal-affairs sergeant / night-shift nurse].
- Primary antagonist: [philanthropist developer / county fixer / studio security chief].
- Pressure beat: [warning beating in a car park / tail on the freeway / shaken down by a detective with a quota].
- Clue objects: [pawn ticket / misdated receipt / cigarette brand mismatch / photograph with wrong shadow angle].
- Moral fulcrum: [expose truth that wrecks a decent person’s life vs. bury a part to stop a worse harm].
- Final image: [neon guttering in rain / a ledger page left on a windowsill / dawn over empty bleachers].

## Micro-Techniques
- City line: “The boulevard ran hot and mean, like a wire carrying the wrong current.”
- Wisecrack as shield: “He smiled like a bill collector. I smiled like a man without chequebook.”
- Simile discipline: one precise, tactile image; avoid stacking.
- Interrogation beat: three moves—soft ask, factual pin, moral jab—then leave.
- Document reveal: one datum that doesn’t fit the timetable (postcode, toll receipt, timestamp).
- Aftermath marker: bruise described later by how a shirt sticks, not by colour.

---

## Self-Audit Checklist
- Does the voice crackle without drowning in similes?
- Are clues fair and human, with red herrings earned by motive?
- Do the women in the case have leverage and designs of their own?
- Is violence brief, improvised, and consequential?
- Does each scene change knowledge, risk, or leverage?
- Does the ending land on a clean image with partial justice and a cost?
