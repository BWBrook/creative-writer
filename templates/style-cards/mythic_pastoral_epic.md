# Style Card: Mythic Pastoral Epic

## Core Idea
An ancient world of layered time—where homely rural life abuts venerable ruins—faces a peril bound up with fate, oaths, and the long memory of peoples. Grandeur sits beside the everyday; moral weight rests on small hands.

## Voice & POV
- Third-person past, flexible distance: from panoramic “chronicle” to close, humane observation.
- Occasional editorial or archival aside (“some say…”, “in later days it was told…”).
- Register: ceremonious yet humane; capable of homely warmth.

## Tone
- Noble, elegiac, and clear-eyed; wonder tempered by the ache of passing things.
- Hope is stubborn, never glib; grief is given its due.
- Humour arises in rustic talk, travel discomforts, and simple pleasures.

## Pacing & Structure
- Road-book cadence: departure → wayfinding → councils and oaths → ordeals (natural and moral) → renunciation/mercy → long, earned denouement.
- Interlacing (entrelacement): follow one company, then another; converge at turning points.
- “Sudden turn toward grace” may arrive at the brink, but consequences still bite.

## World-Building
- Pre-industrial: inns, fords, barrows, watch-towers, craft halls, sea-havens.
- Deep time: annals, genealogies, relics; ruins with remembered names.
- Landscapes are characters: downs, fens, shadowed forests, white mountains, western seas.
- A faint sense of providence is felt, not preached.

## Characters
- Quiet courage in ordinary folk; nobility measured by stewardship and mercy, not raw power.
- High-born guardians with grave courtesy; earthy travellers who love gardens and simple food; craft-proud delvers of stone and metal; sea-seasoned mariners.
- Antagonists are corrupted wills or blind engines of domination; tragedy, not camp.

## Dialogue
- Layered registers:
- High speech for oaths, heraldry, councils.
- Plain, idiomatic talk in inns and by the fire.
- Courtesy formulae, vows, names with epithets; irony is light and respectful.
- Songs and laments appear in-world, advancing lore or mood.

## Diction & Syntax
- British spelling.
- Periodic sentences for ceremony; paratactic “and…and…” chains for march and catalogue.
- Select archaisms (“ere”, “fell”, “thither”) used sparingly and consistently.
- Alliterative touches and balanced pairs (“fair and fell”, “fire and frost”).
- Concrete lexis for craft, weather, and landforms; minimal abstractions.

Narrative Devices
- Framing as translation/edit of older sources; maps, calendars, brief notes.
- Names are meaningful (sound-symbolism, internal phonology rules).
- Verse forms:
- Alliterative long lines for lays and laments.
- Ballad quatrains for marches and tavern songs.
- Appendical feel: short side-notes can gesture to uncharted deeps without derailing the scene.

## Motifs
- Stars, western light, sea-longing; leaves and seed; roads and mile-stones; runes and reforged blades; horns, beacons, and watch-fires; grey ships; rings and oaths; the long defeat vs. long fidelity.

## Moral Stance
- Mercy and pity are strengths; domination corrupts the wielder.
- Renunciation of power can be the highest act.
- Stewardship of the living world outranks glory.

## Common Pitfalls To Avoid
- Pastiching with heavy archaism; modern snark; grimdark cynicism.
- Expository dumps without dramatic need; omniscient theology.
- Quip-driven banter; contemporary slang; anachronistic tech metaphors.

## Ready-to-Paste Prompt

Write an original story in the following style. Do not imitate any specific author by name.

Style: Mythic Pastoral Epic.
Voice & POV: third-person past with flexible distance; ceremonious when speaking of oaths and history, homely when among travellers. Occasional archival aside is permitted.
Setting: pre-industrial secondary world with deep time; rural hamlets, ancient roads, barrows, craft halls, sea-havens. Include maps-in-the-mind: distances, weather, wayfinding.
Plot Shape: departure from a simple life → council or oath-taking → journey through varied terrains → ordeals testing courage and mercy → a renunciation or sparing of an enemy → long denouement that honours loss and return. Use interlaced subplots that rejoin at turning points.
Themes: stewardship over domination; the dignity of small deeds; the ache of passing eras; hope that persists.
Dialogue: layered registers (high speech for vows and counsel; plain speech by the fire). Courtesy, names with epithets, and measured humour.
Diction & Syntax: British spelling; periodic sentences for ceremony; paratactic chains for travel and catalogue; sparing archaism (“ere”, “thither”, “fell”) used consistently; concrete lexis for landforms, weather, craft.
Lore Handling: reveal history through song, inscription, and remembered place-names; gesture to deeper strata without exhaustive exposition.
Verse: include one brief in-world song or lament (alliterative or ballad), advancing mood or lore.
Ending: hard-won, sober hope; costs acknowledged; a sense of things passing into legend.
Shape: each scene advances either (a) the road, (b) the counsel/oath, (c) the moral test, or (d) the world’s time-depth.

## Fill Slots
- Fellowship composition: [quiet rural wanderer; grave guardian; stout craft-worker; sea-wise guide; scholar-messenger].
- Threat: [enshrouding winter from the north; engines felling the greenwood; a will bending others through oaths; a shadow cast from the deeps].
- Waypoints: [barrow field; border inn at a river-ford; watch-tower ridge; echoing craft-hall; white pass; western haven].
- Token & significance: [repaired heirloom; horn of warning; seed from an older land; star-glass; runed shard].
- Moral fulcrum: [spare a beaten foe; forgo using a coercive artefact; speak truth that sunders a claim; relinquish a rightful seat].
- Final image: [grey ship taking the western swell; spring leaves after fire; a garden replanted with heirloom seeds; a hill under stars].

---

## Micro-Techniques
- Catalogue cadence: “They went by hedge and hollow, over stone-ribs and ferny falls, and the wind was from the west.”
- Name with epithet: “Eldric Oak-warden”, “Maelin of the Quiet Harbours”.
- Balanced pairs: “fair and fell”, “stout of heart and spare of word”.
- Allusive aside: “Of that craft the songs speak more fully elsewhere.”

---

## Self-Audit Checklist
- Does ceremony alternate with homely warmth without tonal whiplash?
- Are archaisms restrained and consistent?
- Do landscapes act on mood and choice, not just backdrop?
- Is mercy given narrative power over domination?
- Do songs and names carry lore without stalling pace?
- Does the ending honour cost and return?
