# Style Card: Primeval Sword-and-Sorcery Pulp

## Core Idea
A hardbitten wanderer from the margins—barbarian, corsair, mercenary—collides with decadent city-states, sunken ages, and predatory sorcery. Strength of will and quick steel trump institutions. Civilisation is clever but rotten; the wild is perilous but honest.

## Voice & POV
- Third-person limited past, tight to the protagonist’s senses; occasional panoramic sentence to frame an age or landscape.
- First-person reminiscence is also viable for a grittier confessional feel.
- Register: physical, vivid, lean; rhetorical flourish reserved for scene-setting and moments of awe or revulsion.

## Tone
- Ferocious, vital, fatalistic; contemptuous of pomp and hypocrisy.
- Sardonic humour as release; no quips during mortal stakes.
- Wonder shades into horror; awe is edged with dread.

## Pacing & Structure
- In medias res entry (a brawl, a flight, a theft).
- Chain of reversals: skirmish → intrigue → descent/exploration → weird revelation → desperate gamble → brutal reckoning → curt, ironic denouement.
- Scenes are short; transitions are hard cuts; every scene changes power, knowledge, or stakes.

## World-Building
- Antediluvian or post-imperial patchwork of city-states and frontier wilds: deserts, black jungles, steppe, storm coasts.
- Syncretic cultures with distinct dress, weapons, and oaths; avoid caricature.
- Ruins are “older than memory”: cyclopean stone, star-metal, fossilised gods.
- Sorcery is rare, costly, and sensory: cold winds from nowhere, lotus-dream trances, ash-tanged eclipses. Explanations stay elliptical.

## Characters
- Protagonist: physically formidable, fiercely self-willed; a personal code (keeps bargains; protects the powerless; will not serve tyrants).
- Allies: a wary thief, a learned exile, a sea-wolf captain; all competent, with agency.
- Antagonists: perfumed satraps, priest-sorcerers, revenant kings, inhuman custodians.
- Desire is present but never the plot’s payment; avoid pulp-era objectification.

## Dialogue
- Short, hard, declarative.
- Threats, vows, and prices named plainly.
- Oaths draw on earth and sky, not modern slang.
- No infodumps; world facts leak through argument and bargaining.

## Diction & Syntax
- British spelling.
- Verbs carry weight: hew, grapple, drive, wrench, cast.
- Colour-saturated concrete nouns: iron, jade, pitch, dust, surf, bone.
- Rhythm: staccato 8–15 word lines in action; occasional rolling period for vistas.
- Controlled archaism via word choice, not “ye olde” grammar.

## Information Handling
- History hinted by coinage, tattoos, weapon styles, curses, and murals.
- Magic’s rules inferred from cost and effect; never systematised on the page.
- Keep one or two deep unknowns untouched.

## Combat Principles
- Three-beat exchanges: probe, counter, finish.
- Use terrain and improvised tools; describe breath, footing, torque, blood loss.
- Wounds matter; the hero bleeds and tires; escapes are earned.

## Motifs
- Black lotus and other narcotic blooms; broken idols; moon-bleached bones; brass gongs and drums; sharking gulls; dice on a tavern board; stolen maps; star-iron blades; chained doors with green verdigris.

## Moral Stance
- Personal honour over civic law; mercy for the weak, ruthlessness for tyrants.
- Freedom is dearer than thrones; plunder without cruelty.
- Civilisation and barbarism are masks of conduct, not verdicts of blood.

## Common Pitfalls To Avoid
- Glossy high-fantasy courts; balanced “magic systems”; modern quips.
- Gore porn; sexualised victimhood; moustache-twirling villains.
- Expository lore-dumps; cod-archaic dialogue.

## Ready-to-Paste Prompt

Write an original story in the following style. Do not imitate any specific author by name.

Style: Primeval Sword-and-Sorcery Pulp.
Voice & POV: tight third-person past centred on a formidable wanderer; physical, vivid, lean. Allow occasional panoramic sentence for age/landscape.
Opening: begin in medias res (fight, flight, theft, or bargain turned sour).
Setting: antediluvian/post-imperial patchwork—decadent city-states, frontier wilds, and elder ruins. Distinct cultures without caricature.
Sorcery: rare, costly, sensory. Evoke effect and price; keep rules implied.
Pacing: skirmish → intrigue → descent/exploration → weird revelation → desperate gamble → brutal reckoning → curt, ironic denouement.
Protagonist’s Code: keeps bargains; refuses tyranny; spares the helpless; takes fair loot.
Dialogue: terse; threats, oaths, prices. No exposition speeches.
Diction & Syntax: British spelling; strong verbs; colour-concrete nouns; staccato in action; controlled archaism by lexis, not grammar.
Combat: three-beat exchanges; terrain and improvisation; wounds matter.
Themes: freedom vs. decadence; will against sorcery; the cost of survival.
Ending: survival with a price; treasure lost or spent; a sardonic final image.
Shape: each scene must change power, knowledge, or stakes.

## Fill Slots
- Protagonist archetype: [border scout / corsair / sellsword / hill-tribe raider turned bodyguard].
- Code clause to test: [keep a bargain / protect a child / never kneel to a priest / refuse torture].
- Locale: [desert tomb-city / jungle ziggurat / cliff-built pearl port / steppe caravanserai / glacier-rim valley].
- MacGuffin: [star-iron shard / serpent-script tablet / sea-chart to the black current / idol’s eye].
- Sorcery mode: [lotus-dream necromancy / mirrored doubles / blood-bound glyphs / cold fire from under the earth].
- Antagonist: [perfumed satrap with masked guards / embalmed war-king / high priestess of the tide-wells / scholar who bartered his shadow].
- Ally/foil: [knife-quick thief / exile scholar / sea-wolf captain / spear-sister of the hill tribes].
- Moral fulcrum: [keep the jewel or free the captives / abandon the ally to escape / spare the beaten foe who saved you once].
- Final image: [dawn over bones and sand / a small boat cutting a black swell / coins spilled into an orphan’s hand / a broken blade hammered straight].

---

## Micro-Techniques
- Colour tricolon: “iron, dust, and blood.”
- Hyphenated epithet: “storm-gnawed quay”, “idol-haunted stair”.
- Oath form: “By the [wind, tide, anvil, steppe]…”
- Action cadence: noun-verb chains—“He locked, wrenched, drove.”
- Panoramic pivot: one long sentence to sweep from dunes/coast/jungle to a ruin that dwarfs the cast.
- Economy of lore: one line of coin-age, one mural, one proverb—enough.

---

## Self-Audit Checklist
- Does every scene shift power, knowledge, or stakes?
- Is the prose verb-driven and sensory rather than adjective-stuffed?
- Are combat beats physical and consequential?
- Is sorcery felt (cost, chill, scent) rather than explained?
- Have you kept the feral energy without lapsing into stock pulp contempt for whole peoples or mere decorative women?
- Does the ending cut clean, with price paid and freedom intact (for now)?
