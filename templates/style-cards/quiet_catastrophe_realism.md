# Style Card: Quiet Catastrophe Realism

## Core Idea
Ordinary people meet an extraordinary, plausibly scientific disruption. The voice stays calm, lucid, and practical; dread accrues by understatement rather than spectacle.

## Voice & POV
- First-person past tense is typical; a reflective, capable narrator recounts events after the initial shock.
- Alternative: close third past with limited interiority.
- Register: educated but not ornate; measured irony; no grandstanding.

## Tone
- Understated, stoic, sceptical.
- Quiet dread over gore; moral seriousness without sermonising.
- Occasional dry humour as pressure-release.

## Pacing & Structure
- Slow reveal → reconnaissance → constrained adaptation → moral test → provisional, uneasy equilibrium.
- Early chapters: domestic routine punctured by oddities and conflicting reports.
- Middle: practical logistics (food, water, shelter, travel) and small-group problem-solving.
- Climax: human conflict is as dangerous as the phenomenon.
- Endings: cautious, ambiguous hope; survival, not triumph.

## World-Building
- Present-day or near-past Britain; villages, market towns, modest labs, coastal roads, allotments.
- Institutions are fallible; official communiqués, radio bulletins, rumours.
- Technology is everyday; speculation is restrained and plausible.

## Characters
- Protagonist: observant, self-reliant, not superhuman; likely a professional with practical skills.
- A resourceful partner counters the narrator’s blind spots; competence over bravado.
- Antagonism emerges from fear, parochialism, or opportunism rather than villainy.
- Children or outsiders often embody the future’s ethical claim.

## Dialogue
- Brief, purposeful, edged with understatement.
- Regional or class markers are subtle; avoid caricature.
- Use dialogue to surface ethics and logistics, not exposition dumps.

## Diction & Syntax
- British spelling and idiom.
- Sentences mostly 12–25 words; occasional longer periodic sentence for reflection.
- Figurative language is sparing and concrete; nature and weather supply imagery.
- Prefer precise nouns and plain verbs over adjectives/adverbs.

## Information Handling
- Reveal causes indirectly: partial data, competing hypotheses, observational detail.
- Science is heuristic: what’s measured, what fails, what can be improvised.
- Keep one or two unknowns unresolved to preserve verisimilitude.

## Motifs
- Blindness, silence, fog, tides, seeds/spores, radio static, evacuated streets, hedgerows, church bells, allotments, petrol rationing, makeshift greenhouses.
- Ethical contrasts: neighbourliness vs. tribalism; prudence vs. panic.

## Moral Stance
- Survival entails trade-offs; dignity is measured by care for dependants and strangers.
- Warn against technocratic hubris and romantic fatalism alike.

## Common Pitfalls To Avoid
- Cinematic set-pieces; superheroics; technobabble.
- Neat conspiracies; omniscient explanations; nihilistic endings.
- Modern American slang; anachronistic gadgetry unless justified.

## Ready-to-Paste Prompt

Write an original story in the following style. Do not imitate any specific author by name.

Style: Quiet Catastrophe Realism.
Voice & POV: [first-person past / close third past]; calm, reflective, practical.
Setting: contemporary or near-past Britain; ordinary locales (village, small town, coast).
Speculative Element: a plausibly scientific disruption: [brief description]. Keep explanation partial; privilege observation over theory.
Pacing: slow reveal → reconnaissance → constrained adaptation → moral test → provisional, uneasy equilibrium.
Tone: understated, stoic, quietly ominous; occasional dry humour.
Focus: logistics of survival (food, water, shelter, travel), small-group dynamics, ethical choices.
Characters: competent, non-heroic narrator; a resourceful partner; secondary figures showing varied human responses (cooperation, denial, opportunism).
Dialogue: concise, purposeful, edged with understatement.
Diction & Syntax: British spelling; precise nouns; plain verbs; sparing figurative language anchored in weather and landscape.
Information Handling: conflicting reports, partial measurements, careful inference; leave at least one key unknown unresolved.
Ending: sober, cautiously hopeful; survival, not triumph.
Shape: each scene advances either (a) knowledge of the phenomenon, (b) group cohesion/strain, or (c) resource security.
Do not include cinematic spectacle, neat conspiracy reveals, or omniscient explanation.

- Final image: [greenhouse panes misting before dawn / foghorn and turning tide / allotment with first shoots].

## Self-Audit Checklist
- Does the voice remain calm and practical under stress?
- Are explanations plausible but partial, with competing hypotheses?
- Do scenes privilege logistics and ethics over pyrotechnics?
- Is the diction British, precise, and largely adjective-light?
- Does the ending offer cautious continuity rather than closure?
