# Style Card: Weather-and-Work Adventure Thriller

## Core Idea
A capable professional (skipper, engineer, surveyor, pilot) is pulled into a hazardous mission—salvage, search, survey, extraction—where the real enemies are weather, terrain, and compromised men with financial motives. Authentic procedure and kit matter; integrity costs.

## Voice & POV
- First-person past, practical and observant; allows retrospective clarity without melodrama.
- Alternative: tight third past on a single protagonist.
- Register: technical but readable; dry understatement; emotion shows through decisions, not gush.

## Tone
- Stoic, unsentimental, morally earnest.
- Suspense comes from logistics, weather windows, and machinery limits.
- Occasional bleak humour; no quips under mortal risk.

## Pacing & Structure
- Hook via duty/legacy/debt (a letter, a will, a wreck report) → legwork (permits, crew, gear) → transit into a hard place → escalating environmental ordeals → human double-cross revealed under pressure → narrow, plausible survival or salvage → sober aftermath.
- Set-pieces are physical: storm run, white-out traverse, reef approach, shaft collapse, fuel-starvation glide.
- Each chapter advances either plan, position, weather, or trust.

## World-Building
- Real-world or lightly fictionalised geographies: fjords, estuaries, pack-ice leads, volcano flanks, desert rigs, derelict mines.
- Institutions: harbour boards, insurers, survey offices, customs, local clans.
- Infrastructure and kit are named and used correctly: winches, pumps, sextants/GPS, echo-sounders, pit-props, bulldozers, flare pistols.

## Characters
- Protagonist: skilled, stubborn, not superhuman; carries a scar or debt.
- Allies: weathered locals, taciturn riggers, a pilot with judgement, a scientist with necessary data; each with agency and limits.
- Antagonists: financiers, fixers, smugglers, reckless executives; greedy but plausible.
- The environment functions as a character—indifferent and stronger than anyone’s plan.

## Dialogue
- Terse, transactional, jargon-aware.
- Bargains, risks, and weather windows are stated plainly.
- Local idiom used lightly; avoid caricature.

## Diction & Syntax
- British spelling.
- Concrete nouns and strong, literal verbs; minimal simile.
- Mix of crisp 10–15-word lines for procedure with occasional long sentence to render a coastline, storm front, or ice-pressure sequence.

## Information Handling
- Show competence by doing: checklists, gauge readings, knots, charts, load plans.
- Technical detail is accurate and functional; never decorative.
- Reveal schemes through invoices, logbooks, manifests, and slips in testimony, not villain monologues.

## Action & Risk Principles
- Physics obeyed: mass, momentum, drag, fatigue, cold.
- Equipment fails realistically; redundancy and improvisation are earned.
- Injuries impair; recovery alters subsequent choices.

## Motifs
- Falling barometer; isobars tight on the chart; green water over the bow; ice screeching on plating; winch-wire singing; diesel cough; sulphur reek; shaft timbers popping; flare against sleet; a dog-eared pilot book; a ruined cairn.

## Moral Stance
- Integrity over profit; respect for local knowledge and the sea/land.
- Courage is work sustained under fear, not theatrics.
- Rescue and stewardship outrank treasure—at a price.

## Common Pitfalls To Avoid
- Spy-glamour, superheroics, miraculous saves.
- Expository dumps detached from task.
- Paper-thin sidekicks, decorative women, or patronising guide figures; everyone on the page should earn their keep by judgement and work.
- Tech fetishism without consequence.

## Ready-to-Paste Prompt

Write an original modern adventure in the following style. Do not imitate any specific author by name.

Style: Weather-and-Work Adventure Thriller.
Voice & POV: first-person past from a competent professional; dry understatement; emotion through decisions and procedure.
Premise: a hazardous mission ([salvage/search/survey/extraction]) triggered by [will/letter/insurance claim/wreck report].
Setting: harsh real-world geography ([Arctic fjord / North Atlantic reef / Andean mine / desert oilfield / sub-Antarctic island]) with named infrastructure and kit used correctly.
Structure: duty hook → legwork (permits, crew, gear) → transit into danger → escalating environmental ordeals → human double-cross under pressure → plausible, hard-won outcome → sober aftermath.
Focus: logistics, weather windows, machinery limits, crew dynamics; the environment as primary antagonist.
Dialogue: terse, technical, price-aware; bargains and risks stated plainly; no villain speeches.
Diction & Syntax: British spelling; concrete nouns; literal, strong verbs; procedural paragraphs interleaved with longer landscape/weather sentences.
Information Handling: reveal schemes via documents (manifests, logbooks, invoices) and operational anomalies; keep technical detail accurate and functional.
Themes: integrity vs. profit; respect for local expertise; competence under pressure; the price of rescue.
Ending: survival or salvage with cost; legal or ethical consequences acknowledged; no triumphalism.
Shape: each scene must advance plan, position, weather, or trust.
Guardrails: physics obeyed; no miraculous saves; competence distributed among the cast.

## Fill Slots
- Protagonist: [trawler skipper / structural engineer / bush pilot / hydrographic surveyor / mining geologist].
- Debt/obligation: [inheritance clause / unpaid salvage lien / promise to a dead crewmate / insurance audit].
- Locale: [Lofoten winter / Bass Strait blow / Atacama high mine / Greenland pack-ice / Scottish sea loch].
- Vehicle/kit: [seine-net trawler / Beaver floatplane / tracked dozer / side-scan sonar / winch-rig with A-frame].
- Hazard: [katabatic storm / ice pressure / rogue tide race / methane pocket / avalanche].
- Antagonist: [port fixer with falsified manifests / insurer pushing a denial / executive cutting corners / smuggler using the wreck].
- Local ally: [harbour pilot / Inuit hunter / rig medic / lighthouse keeper / union diver].
- Paper trail clue: [duplicate bill of lading / altered logbook time / sealed crate weight anomaly / survey datum mismatch].
- Moral fulcrum: [abandon salvage to rescue rivals / expose fraud that bankrupts innocent workers / ground the aircraft to save a whale-calf corridor].
- Final image: [barometer rising over a stripped deck / wreck light winking out at dawn / hands black with oil rinsed in snowmelt / court stamp on a dog-eared logbook].

## Micro-Techniques
- Weather line: “The glass fell a quarter-inch before noon; by two the fjord had white eyes.”
- Procedural paragraph: three to five sentences that walk a task—rigging a tow, splicing wire, plotting a fix—without grandstanding.
- Document reveal: one clean sentence on a manifest or log that doesn’t fit the tide tables.
- Negotiation beat: terse price/penalty exchange—deadline, tonnage, weather window.
- Fatigue marker: breath, finger-numbness, grease in the cut: consequences persist into the next scene.
- Landscape period: a single long sentence to take in the reef line, the shoal, the swell sets, and the only safe run.

---

## Self-Audit Checklist
- Does the environment drive risk more than moustache-twirling villains?
- Are technical details accurate, earned, and tied to action?
- Do weather, fatigue, and equipment limits shape choices?
- Is competence shown through procedure rather than declared?
- Do the secondary characters earn the page through skill, leverage, and local knowledge?
- Does the ending acknowledge cost and consequence without cheap cynicism?
